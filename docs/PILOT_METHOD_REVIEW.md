## What the two pilots established

### 1. Source JSON is evidence, not editorial paragraphing

The `paragraphs` arrays are extraction units. They can contain repeated headings, page joins, broken words, spacing/OCR anomalies, sentences split across elements, verse embedded inside prose, and non-authorial Wikisource maintenance material.

Therefore every chapter begins with source review and a source-note sidecar before T1.

### 2. Translation fidelity has four simultaneous dimensions

A successful English chapter must preserve:

1. factual content;
2. Kalaignar's sequence of thought;
3. rhetorical action — questions, repetition, accumulation, punchlines and contrasts;
4. voice — oral cadence, political force, affection, humour, irony and concrete imagery.

English fluency is a gate, not permission to erase these dimensions.

### 3. T1, T2 and T3 do different jobs

- **T1** accounts for authorial Tamil and establishes a complete meaning draft.
- **T2** is the decisive Tamil-English Kalaignar-voice review. It is where flattened idiom, softened political force, over-interpretation and cultural mistranslation are corrected.
- **T3** asks whether the revised translation works as English while preserving deliberate strangeness that belongs to Kalaignar.

T3 must not hide a source problem merely to improve smoothness.

### 4. Thought structure is part of fidelity

Both pilots showed that Kalaignar's sequence itself carries meaning. The structural audit therefore remains mandatory after T3.

### 5. Existing English is never drafting authority

Where earlier English exists, it is consulted only after the fresh translation has passed the structural gate. A better phrase may be adopted only with an explicit comparison note.

### 6. Concrete imagery survives even when unfamiliar

Pilot 1 established this through `உயிர் எமக்கு வெல்லமல்ல`: unfamiliar imagery should not be abstracted merely for smooth English.

### 7. Cultural vocabulary must not be guessed from scene

Pilot 2 established this through `போகசாலை`, where the first intuitive English over-interpreted the term. Lexical/contextual evidence must precede confident translation of unfamiliar vocabulary.

### 8. Embedded verse needs its own fidelity discipline

Songs remain verse. Preserve lineation where recoverable, speaker/target, social attack, image, humour and sequence before attempting elegance or rhyme.

### 9. Source uncertainty has distinct classes

Production must distinguish:

- **textual-reading uncertainty** — what characters/words the source actually contains is unstable;
- **semantic-interpretation uncertainty** — the visible text is stable but its exact meaning/syntax remains uncertain;
- **contextual extraction anomaly** — an extracted form is clearly defective but surrounding syntax establishes the intended function;
- **accepted scholarly uncertainty** — after reasonable review, a stable source expression remains semantically uncertain and is preserved transparently rather than guessed.

Accepted scholarly uncertainty is not the same as silently ignoring a hold. It requires a dedicated editorial decision, visible final treatment, and a future-revision path if stronger evidence emerges.

### 10. Non-authorial material is excluded explicitly

Wikisource maintenance notices, duplicated interface text, or other editorial artefacts do not enter Kalaignar's English prose. Their exclusion must remain recorded.

## Production workflow

For each chapter:

`source review → source notes → T1 → T2 → T3 → thought-structure audit → terminology/cultural audit → post-freeze comparator if one exists → approval`

A source-dependent hold interrupts approval but does not necessarily prevent non-source-dependent review gates if the uncertain area is isolated and clearly marked.

## Production batching rule

Normal production batch size remains **5 chapters**. However, a five-chapter batch is a **management envelope**, not a command to translate five chapters in one enormous iteration.

Within the batch:

- process one chapter at a time;
- complete its source review before translating it;
- make durable, reviewable commits as gates complete;
- do not carry hidden source-cleaning assumptions from one chapter to another;
- do not start a sixth chapter until the five-chapter batch is closed;
- reduce a batch to 1–3 chapters when source complexity or chapter length requires it.
