# Nenjukku Needhi — Translation Glossary

This is a living glossary. Entries are added only after reading the Tamil in context. No term is mechanically frozen merely because it has appeared once.

## Principles

- Preserve political and emotional force.
- Prefer Kalaignar's relationship to a person or idea over detached encyclopedic naming when the Tamil uses an epithet/form of address.
- Transliteration, translation and first-use glossing are all available; choose according to context.
- Do not silently replace Kalaignar's Tamil rendering of a quotation with an external canonical English version.
- Record meaningful changes of decision here so later chapters remain consistent.
- Historical/dialectal Tamil must be resolved from speaker and context before a modern surface meaning is chosen.
- A culturally specific gesture or formula may remain transliterated with a minimal functional gloss when a generic English replacement would erase what the Tamil is doing.
- Historical proper names and institutional titles may be normalised to their securely attested English forms when literal translation would misidentify the entity; such normalisation must be disclosed in source notes.

## Working entries

| Tamil | Working English policy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `அண்ணா` | **Anna** | Retain as a living form of address/name when Kalaignar uses it personally or politically; do not mechanically expand to C. N. Annadurai. |
| `அறிஞர் அண்ணா` | **Arignar Anna** (working) | Preserve epithet unless context strongly favours an explanatory first-use gloss. Review across later chapters. |
| `பேரறிஞர் அண்ணா` | **Perarignar Anna** | `v1-ch05`: preserve the intensified political/literary epithet rather than flattening it to the bare personal name; the following `C. N. Annadurai, M.A.` is retained where Kalaignar himself supplies it. |
| `தந்தை பெரியார்` | **Thanthai Periyar** (working) | Preserve the political/honorific force; first-use explanatory gloss may be considered in reader-facing editions. |
| `காந்தியடிகள்` | Contextual: **Gandhiji / Gandhi** | Tamil carries reverential address. Use a form that preserves register without making English unnatural. |
| `அம்மா` / `அப்பா` | **Amma / Appa** or **my mother / my father** contextually | Kinship voice is important. Avoid rigid one-to-one replacement. |
| `தாலி` | **thali** with first-use contextual gloss where needed | Culturally specific marital ornament/symbol; avoid flattening to simply “necklace”. |
| `சமுதாய நீதி` / `சமூக நீதி` | **social justice** | Check historical/political context. |
| `பூரண சுயராஜ்யம்` | **complete independence / Purna Swaraj** contextually | Preserve historical terminology where politically salient. |
| `தீண்டாமை` | **untouchability** | Use historically direct term. |
| `மொழிப் போர்` / language-protection struggle | Contextual **language struggle / language war** | Preserve movement/struggle force; avoid weak paraphrases such as “language issue”. `v1-ch05` retains the war/battle vocabulary around the 1938 anti-Hindi agitation. |
| `பிற்படுத்தப்பட்ட` | **backward communities / backward classes** contextually | Historical/social category. Avoid renderings such as “backward masses” when they sound like the translator's insult rather than the category Kalaignar is invoking. |
| `புனுகு` | **punugu / civet perfume** contextually | Preserve the concrete fragrant substance in metaphors; do not abstract it to “cosmetic” or “comfort”. |
| `அரிநமோத்து சிந்தம்` | **Arinamothu Sindham**, with minimal gloss **old initiation formula** where needed | `v1-ch03`: externally attested in older Tamil education-initiation practice. Retain Kalaignar's Tamilised form; do not silently Sanskritise it or impose a speculative literal doctrinal translation. |
| `நோக்கு` (dated Brahmin-Tamil pronoun) | Contextual **for/to you** when functioning as `உனக்கு` | `v1-ch03`/`v1-ch04`: dated Brahmin-Tamil usage. Preserve dialectal meaning without inventing an English regional accent. |
| `உச்சிமோந்து` / `உச்சிமோத்தல்` | **smell the crown of the head in affection** | A specific affectionate gesture, especially toward a child. Preserve the gesture when narratively important rather than silently substituting a generic kiss. |
| `தென்னிந்திய நல உரிமைச் சங்கம்` | **South Indian Liberal Federation (SILF)** | `v1-ch04`: use the historically attested English proper name rather than a literal English rendering of the Tamil organisation name. Popularly known as the Justice Party after its English daily *Justice*. Record the normalisation in source notes. |
| `நீதிக் கட்சி` / `ஜஸ்டிஸ் கட்சி` | **Justice Party** | Historical proper name. Retain the established English name rather than alternating between literal `Justice Party` and ad hoc transliteration. |
| `சுயமரியாதை இயக்கம்` | **Self-Respect Movement** | Established historical movement name. Do not weaken to `self-esteem movement`. |
| `பகுத்தறிவு` | **rationalism** | In the Periyar/Self-Respect political context, preserve the ideological term rather than generic `reason` or `reasoning`. |
| `தாழ்த்தப்பட்ட மக்கள்` | **depressed people / people from the depressed communities** contextually | Historical vocabulary associated with the period's `Depressed Classes`; do not modernise the memoir's wording into a later administrative category without reason. |
| `முதல் மந்திரி` | **First Minister** in the dyarchy-era Madras Presidency context | `v1-ch04`/`v1-ch05`: period office title for the 1920–1930 governments and the Raja of Bobbili's 1932 succession. Do not automatically modernise to `Chief Minister`. |
| `முதல்வர்` / `முதலமைச்சர்` | Historical office by context; **Premier** for Rajaji's 1937 Madras Presidency office | `v1-ch05`: constitutional period matters. Do not mechanically reuse `First Minister` or modern `Chief Minister` when the attested 1937 office is Premier. |
| `மந்திரி சபை` | **ministry** / **ministry (cabinet)** contextually | Preserve period political vocabulary; `ministry` is preferred in historical narrative unless institutional clarity requires otherwise. |
| `சட்டசபைத் தலைவர்` | **President of the Legislative Council** in the 1920s Madras Presidency context | Historical institutional normalisation in `v1-ch04`: the relevant body was the Madras Legislative Council. Do not use modern `Legislative Assembly` for this period. |
| `இந்தியா மந்திரி` | **Secretary of State for India** when referring to the British India Office | `v1-ch04`: Kalaignar's Tamil names Montagu by function; use the securely attested official English office title rather than the unnatural literal `India Minister`. Record the normalisation in source notes. |
| `சுயாட்சி` | **self-government** / **Swaraj** contextually | Use `self-government` for a general institutional demand; use `Swaraj Party` when the Tamil names the historical party `சுயாட்சிக் கட்சி`. |
| `சொற்போர்` | **debate / verbal debate** contextually | Preserve the active oral character; avoid flattening to `discussion` where the rhetorical contest matters. |
| `வடமொழி` | **Sanskrit** when used in its established literary/linguistic sense | `v1-ch05`: University of Madras Lexicon gives `வடமொழி` as Sanskrit language. Do not force the overly literal `northern language` when the lexicalised sense is clear. |
| `இறும்பூது` | **wonder / amazement** contextually | `v1-ch05`: preserve the literary sense. Do not add `pride` merely because the surrounding autobiographical moment is emotionally resonant. |
| `தமிழர் தலைவர்` | **Leader of the Tamils** when functioning as epithet or transparent translated title | `v1-ch05`: preserve the deliberate echo between Periyar being accepted as `தமிழர் தலைவர்` and Sami Chidambaranar's biography bearing the same phrase. |
| `ஆணித்தரமான` | **firm / forceful / strong** contextually | `v1-ch05`: `ஆணித்தரம்` is lexicalised as firmness/strength/quality. Do not manufacture an explicit nail-driving image from the word's form. |

## Terms awaiting evidence from wider corpus

The following should not be permanently fixed until more chapters are read:

- `திராவிட இயக்கம்`
- `இன உணர்வு`
- `தமிழினம்`
- movement slogans and recurring political epithets
- later-period institutional uses of `மாநிலம்`, `சட்டசபை`, `முதல்வர்`, and related office vocabulary outside the currently reviewed historical settings
