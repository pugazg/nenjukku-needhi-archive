## English-only findings and source-checked revisions

### 1. Tanjore conference syntax

T2: `At the Tanjore conference held in connection with it...`

This is grammatical but stiff.

Tamil: `அதன் தொடர்பாகத் தஞ்சையில் கூடிய மாநாட்டில்...`

**T3 revision:** `At the conference convened in Tanjore in connection with it...`

The relation to the no-confidence resolution is unchanged.

### 2. Intervening ministers

T2: `also served as ministers.`

Tamil uses `பொறுப்பேற்றனர்`, an assumption-of-office verb.

**T3 revision:** `also took office as ministers.`

This is both more natural and closer to the Tamil action.

### 3. Compulsory-Hindi law

T2: `The moment the law making Hindi compulsory came into being...`

Tamil: `'இந்திகட்டாயம்' என்று சட்டம் பிறந்தவுடன்...`

The birth metaphor is ordinary Tamil legislative phrasing here; preserving it literally makes English cumbersome.

**T3 revision:** `As soon as the law making Hindi compulsory was enacted...`

No political force is reduced.

### 4. Agitation sentence

T2: `They conducted agitations.`

Tamil: `கிளர்ச்சிகள் நடத்தினர்.`

**T3 revision:** `They organized agitations.`

The historically political noun `agitations` is retained; only the English collocation is improved.

### 5. Red-flame image

T2: `Through the newspapers they poured out condemnations that rose like red flame.`

Tamil: `செந்தீயென எழும் தங்கள் கண்டனங்களைச் செய்தித் தாள்களின் வாயிலாக வெளியிட்டுக் குவித்தனர்.`

**T3 revision:** `Through the newspapers they poured forth condemnations that rose like a red flame.`

The `செந்தீ` image remains concrete; the change repairs English article/cadence only.

### 6. Congress Tamils' inward opposition

T2: `did not inwardly favour compulsory Hindi.`

Tamil: `கட்டாய இந்தியை உள்ளூர விரும்பவில்லை.`

**T3 revision:** `did not, at heart, favour compulsory Hindi.`

`at heart` carries the inward/private force naturally in English.

### 7. Panneerselvam response

T2: `All Tamil Nadu heard that reply, praised Panneerselvam and rejoiced.`

Tamil: `அந்தப் பதில் உரை கேட்டுத் தமிழகமே பன்னீர் செல்வத்தைப் பாராட்டி மகிழ்ந்தது.`

**T3 revision:** `Hearing that reply, all Tamil Nadu praised Panneerselvam and rejoiced.`

The personification `all Tamil Nadu` is retained.

### 8. Maraimalai Adigal consolation sentence

T2: `The anti-Hindi movement brought great consolation to Maraimalai Adigal, who had grieved in this way.`

Tamil: `...என்று ஏங்கிய மறைமலையடிகளாருக்கு இந்தி எதிர்ப்பு இயக்கம் பெரும் ஆறுதலை அளித்தது.`

**T3 revision:** `For Maraimalai Adigal, who lamented thus, the anti-Hindi movement brought great consolation.`

The lament/consolation relationship is unchanged, but the English moves more cleanly.

### 9. Fourteen-year pivot

T2: `Between 1924 and 1938 lay fourteen years.`

Tamil: `1924-க்கும் 1938'க்கும் இடையே 14 ஆண்டுகள்.`

**T3 revision:** `Fourteen years lay between 1924 and 1938.`

The emphatic chronological pivot remains the opening of its paragraph.

### 10. Nehru's Europe sentence

T2: `Pandit Nehru went to Europe for rest.`

Tamil: `பண்டித நேரு ஓய்விற்காக ஐரோப்பா சென்று விட்டார்.`

**T3 revision:** `Pandit Nehru went to Europe to rest.`

No factual or tonal change.

### 11. Government-budget resolution

T2: `Money from the government budget, which consists of the people's money, must not be spent on the unnecessary Hindi language.`

Tamil: `பொதுமக்கள் பணத்தைக் கொண்ட சர்க்கார் வரவு செலவுத் திட்டத்தில் அவசியமற்ற இந்தி மொழிக்காகப் பணம் செலவழிக்கக் கூடாது.`

**T3 revision:** `Public money in the government budget must not be spent on the unnecessary Hindi language.`

The public-money argument remains explicit while the English loses an awkward relative clause.

### 12. Anna reveal fragment

T2: `Back then: C. N. Annadurai, M.A.`

Tamil: `அன்றைக்கு சி.என். அண்ணாதுரை எம்.ஏ.`

**T3 revision:** `In those days: C. N. Annadurai, M.A.`

The deliberate fragment and before-fame contrast remain intact.

### 13. World-values sentence

T2: `were subjected to severe challenge!`

Tamil: `கடுமையான அறைகூவலுக்கு ... ஆளான ஆண்டு அது!`

**T3 revision:** `came under severe challenge!`

This removes bureaucratic passive phrasing while preserving the repeated `it was a year` structure and intensity.

### 14. Reported criticism of Periyar

T2: `He has taken anti-Hindi agitation as a weapon with which to fulfil his passionate goal of destroying the Congress...`

Tamil: `காங்கிரசை ஒழிக்க வேண்டும் என்ற தன் ஆர்வம் நிறைந்த குறிக்கோளை நிறைவேற்றிக் கொள்ள இந்தி எதிர்ப்பை ஒரு கருவியாகக் கொண்டுள்ளார்...`

**T3 revision:** `He has made the anti-Hindi agitation a weapon for fulfilling his fervent aim of destroying the Congress...`

The charge remains explicitly reported, not narrator-endorsed.

### 15. Anna's physical-description fragment

T2: `A short figure!`

Tamil: `குள்ளமான உருவம்!`

**T3 revision:** `Short in stature!`

This reads naturally as a crowd exclamation while retaining the fragmentary chorus.

## Deliberately retained unusual English

T3 explicitly **does not** smooth the following:

- `I cannot agree, letter for letter...` — preserves Kalaignar's Tamil-mediated Voltaire wording rather than importing a canonical English quotation;
- `became corpses within the prison walls` — preserves the blunt force of `பிணமாயினர்`;
- `The Tamil dipped the flag...` — preserves the collective personification in `தமிழன்`;
- the flag dipped in the heroes' blood and flown until it touches the sky — preserves the source image;
- `How the man speaks!` — retains the colloquial audience voice;
- pomegranate pearls, opened sluice, Courtallam waterfall and Tamil `chindu` — remain separate concrete images;
- `It was written. It was said.` — preserves Kalaignar's clipped repetition and reported-speech framing.
