## Minor English revisions

### 1. Memory paragraph

Current: `the emotion-filled sorrow mingled with joy`

Issue: grammatical but heavy and abstract in English.

Revision: `the sorrow, steeped in feeling and mingled with joy`

Reason: improves English cadence while preserving the simultaneous joy/sorrow structure.

### 2. Muneeswaran punchline

Current: `When his own lot is hard enough, how can he come to help others?`

Revision: `When he himself is in such straits, how can he come to help others?`

Reason: more idiomatic English, same comic personification and rhetorical question.

### 3. Two Avvaiyars

Current: `Bent and shrunken like cats until the day they died...`

Revision: `Hunched and shrunken like cats until the day they died...`

Reason: `hunched` is more natural for the bodily image without changing it.

### 4. Family reaction

Current: `my mother and sisters swelled with happiness`

Revision: `my mother and sisters brimmed with happiness`

Reason: avoids an unintended physical image in English while retaining `பூரித்துப் போனார்கள்` as overflowing joy.

### 5. `முத்துமாரி பொழிந்து`

Current: `Showering me with kisses, mothers and elders had blessed me...`

Revision: `Raining kisses upon me, mothers and elders had blessed me...`

Reason: restores Kalaignar's concrete rain-of-kisses image rather than smoothing it into a generic English idiom.

## Deliberate features retained

- `Like a ball that springs away with greater speed when kicked.` remains a fragment because the Tamil uses the image as a sharp rhetorical beat before the next staccato sentences.
- `punugu` remains visible; replacing it with generic perfume alone would erase the concrete image.
- `Arinamothu Sindham` remains in Kalaignar's Tamilised form with only the minimal functional gloss established at T2.
- `smelled the crown of my head in affection` remains intentionally culture-specific rather than becoming a generic kiss/hug.
- the mantra remains transliterated and is not silently normalised into a standard Sanskrit form.
- `big men`, `bondage`, `backward communities`, and `oppressed people` retain the social/political hierarchy and force established at T2.
- village dialogue is naturalised only enough to be intelligible; no invented English regional dialect is used.
