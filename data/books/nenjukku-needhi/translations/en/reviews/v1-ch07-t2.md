## Review method

The complete pinned Tamil chapter was compared against T1 with the P0 controls enforced. The page-51 Jinnah join and page-53/54 `கொண்- / டிருந்தது` join remain continuous; `முஸ்லீம் வீக்கிற்கும்` and `பள்ளிக்கூடத்திலிருத்த` are translated only by their secure contextual functions; the stray extraction backtick is ignored; Alagiri and Chittarasu remain separate people; the authored 1939 M. N. Roy chronology and the fifteen-year-old / four-months-before-`Murasoli` tension are preserved rather than corrected.

The T2 aim is not to turn pages 51–53 into a modern history textbook. Their function is to build a large political field and then suddenly reveal that one strand of that field reaches a thin schoolboy named Karunanidhi.

## Findings and revisions

### 1. English title and `மாணவ நேசன்`

T1: **Are You the One Who Runs 'Maanava Nesan'?**

The title keeps the standalone question in the Tamil and preserves `மாணவ நேசன்` as a publication name rather than reducing it to a generic “student magazine.”

**Action:** retain the title and **Maanava Nesan** as the working romanized publication name for this chapter. The precise corpus-wide romanisation may be revisited at the terminology gate.

The visitor's embedded question is not identical to the title and must remain distinct.

### 2. Gandhi–Jinnah exchange

T1 correctly preserves direct dialogue and `ஜனாப் ஜின்னா` as **Janab Jinnah**.

The reply `தேசியம் ஒருவருடைய தனிச் சொத்து அல்ல` is compact and aphoristic.

**T2 revision:** **“Nationalism is not anyone's private property.”**

This is closer to the Tamil negative construction than “Nationalism is no one's private property” while preserving its force.

### 3. Prophet Muhammad celebrations and Dravidian–League bond

T1's “celebrations of the Prophet Muhammad” is understandable but stiff.

**T2 revision:** **celebrations held in honour of the Prophet Muhammad**.

The final sentence should retain Kalaignar's retrospective claim of a continuing bond.

**T2 revision:** **It would be no exaggeration to say that this relationship helped forge the strong bond that would continue between the Dravidian movement and the Muslim League.**

No external claim is substituted for Kalaignar's own retrospective judgment.

### 4. 1938 India / China / Europe / Burma widening

The source deliberately moves across multiple geographical scales and says China's internal disorder and foreign invasion were `ஒன்றையொன்று மிஞ்சிக் கொண்டிருந்தன`.

T1's “seemed to outdo one another” preserves this unusual image.

**Action:** retain.

The war-cloud image `யுத்தக் கருமேகங்கள் குமுறிச் சூழ்ந்தன` also remains visible as **dark clouds of war gathered and rumbled**.

### 5. 1939 language-struggle victory

T1:
> We may therefore count 1939 itself as the year of the first-stage victory in the language struggle.

The source includes the narrator's collective `நாம் எண்ணிக் கொள்ளலாம்`; this should remain audible.

**T2 revision:** **We may therefore regard 1939 itself as the year of our first-stage victory in the language struggle.**

The authored Rajaji/interim-ministry chronology remains untouched.

### 6. `அகில இந்திய காங்கிரஸ் கட்சி`

This chapter is clearly referring to the historical Indian National Congress. The project's established practice permits secure historical proper-name normalization.

**T2 revision:** **Indian National Congress** instead of **All India Congress Party**.

This is entity-name normalization, not source correction.

### 7. Tripuri / Subhas contest

`மீண்டும் தலைவராக ... போட்டி விட்டார்` is more naturally the act of standing for the presidency than “entering the contest to become president.”

`நேரு தடுத்தும்` is also better understood as Nehru trying to dissuade him.

**T2 revisions:**

- **Subhas Bose stood for the presidency again**;
- **Jawaharlal Nehru ... tried to dissuade him, but Subhas would not listen**.

The repeated source form **Subhas** / **Subhas Chandra Bose** is retained.

### 8. Rajendra Prasad sequence

The source surface is awkward: `அடுத்து காந்தியடிகளின் அன்பைப் பெற்றார், ராசேந்திர பிரசாத்...`

The secure function is clear: Rajendra Prasad, favoured by Gandhi, was next elected Congress president.

**T2 revision:** **Rajendra Prasad, who enjoyed Gandhiji's affection, was then elected president of the Congress.**

No unattested Tamil correction is asserted.

### 9. Britain, crisis and satyagraha

T1's “the British government, which was suffering in expectation of war in Europe” is too literal.

Tamil: `ஐரோப்பாவில் போரை எதிர்பார்த்து அல்லல்பட்டுக் கொண்டிருந்த ஆங்கிலேய அரசாங்கம்`.

**T2 revision:** **the British government, already troubled by the prospect of war in Europe**.

The second sentence should retain the opportunism argument clearly:

**T2 revision:** **Gandhiji's argument was that taking advantage of the opportunity to oppose England while it was caught in crisis would violate the principles of satyagraha.**

### 10. Congress Working Committee continuity

After explicitly naming the `காங்கிரஸ் காரியக் கமிட்டி`, the next paragraph refers back to `காங்கிரஸ் கமிட்டி`.

**T2 revision:** use **Congress Working Committee** in that linked reference as well, so the English does not accidentally suggest a different body.

### 11. Newspaper recollection and 105-degree fever

The T1 passage preserves both the physical extremity and the political stubbornness. The quoted newspaper sentence remains distinct and the stray source backtick is correctly absent.

**Action:** retain.

The unusual **105 degrees** is Kalaignar's wording and is not converted, qualified or fact-checked inside the memoir.

### 12. `கவிக்குயில் சரோஜினி தேவி`

T1 uses **the poet-songbird Sarojini Devi**.

The epithet `கவிக்குயில்` is active praise, and the source itself names `சரோஜினி தேவி`. T2 should not silently replace the source name with an external biographical form.

**Action:** retain **the poet-songbird Sarojini Devi** and preserve the quoted **first thirty years** exactly as authored.

### 13. M. N. Roy's party

The Tamil says `புரட்சி ஜனநாயகக் கட்சி` and places its founding in 1939. P0 records that the historically established English party name is **Radical Democratic Party**, while reference chronology commonly dates that party to 1940.

Under the project's proper-name rule, the entity name may be normalized while the memoir's date must not be corrected.

**T2 revision:** **It was in this very year, 1939, that M. N. Roy started the Radical Democratic Party.**

The **1939** remains exactly Kalaignar's authored chronology.

### 14. Macro-history → schoolboy reveal

T1:
> One man engaged in such efforts wanted to meet me while I was still a schoolboy.

This safely resolves `பள்ளிக்கூடத்திலிருத்த` without inventing corrected Tamil.

The following `எண்ணிக் கொண்டிருந்திருக்கிறார்` has an inferential “must have imagined” force.

**T2 revision:** **He must have imagined that a person called Karunanidhi would be roughly his own age and build.**

Retain the comic payoff: **a thin fifteen-year-old youth**.

### 15. Visitor's question

Tamil:
> `நீங்கள்தானா 'மாணவ நேசன்' நடத்துகிற கருணாநிதி`

T1 adds **really**, which is not needed; the doubt is already narrated immediately before the quotation.

**T2 revision:** **“Are you Karunanidhi, the one who runs 'Maanava Nesan'?”**

The title remains the separate standalone **Are You the One Who Runs 'Maanava Nesan'?**

### 16. Bharathidasan's spinning-wheel sarcasm

The Tamil deliberately repeats `பைத்தியக்காரத்தனம்` in a balanced comparison.

T1:
> Just as mad as believing that the country will advance through the spinning wheel is this handwritten magazine.

This is grammatically possible but does not reproduce the parallel punch.

**T2 revision:** **“If believing that the country will advance through the spinning wheel is madness, this handwritten magazine is just as mad.”**

The spinning-wheel reference remains explicit; no explanatory political aside is inserted into Bharathidasan's line.

### 17. Handwritten journal vocabulary

The source moves among `கையெழுத்து ஏடு`, `கையெழுத்துப் பத்திரிகை` and `கையெழுத்துப் பிரதி`.

In this chapter, English should distinguish the **journal/publication** from its individual **copies**.

**T2 policy:**

- use **handwritten journal** for the publication form;
- use **copy / copies** when the physical fifty-copy production run is meant.

This avoids the T1 phrase “handwritten copies give students...” sounding like the individual object rather than the form of student publishing.

**T2 revision:** **It is my belief that handwritten journals give students a good opportunity to refine and polish their thoughts.**

### 18. `சிறுதுளி பெருவெள்ளம்`

T1's **Little drops make a great flood.** keeps the compact water image and supports the chapter's later current/depth ending.

**Action:** retain. Do not replace it with an explanatory proverb paraphrase.

### 19. `பொங்கல் மலர்`

T1's **Pongal special issue** correctly treats `மலர்` as a publication form rather than a literal flower.

**Action:** retain.

### 20. `டெம்மி பேப்பர்` and `இனாம்!`

The concrete material/economic detail is central to the memoir of becoming a publisher.

T1 correctly retains **demy paper** and the joke **The labour and the articles were free!**

**Action:** retain both. Do not generalize this to “production costs.”

### 21. Birth of `Murasoli`

The source makes a practical copying problem produce a publishing innovation:

fifty handwritten copies → “what else can be done?” → `முரசொலி` → **not** weekly/monthly → `துண்டு வெளியீடுகளாக!`.

T1 preserves this causal chain.

For `துண்டு வெளியீடுகள்`, **leaflet issues** keeps both the physical form and the serial/publication sense better than generic “pamphlets.”

**Action:** retain **As leaflet issues!**

### 22. Recruiter's appeal — `பாசறை`

T1's **As a camp for that purpose** is overly literal in current English, but the source's movement-camp image should not disappear.

**T2 revision:** **“A Students' Federation has been formed as an organizing camp for that purpose.”**

Retain the martial/organizational sequence:

**students united → form ranks → raise their voices → organizing camp → organizer → members**.

### 23. Delayed Communist reveal

The source first makes the khadi shirt and praise of national leaders suggest Congress, then overturns that expectation: the visitor is not a Congressman; only later does the narrator say he did not know the man behind the scenes was a Communist.

**Action:** retain the delay. Do not identify a specific national student organization or the visitor by name without source evidence.

### 24. Freedom – Peace – Equality

`சுதந்திரம் - சமாதானம் - சமத்துவம்` functions as a three-beat emotional slogan.

**Action:** retain **Freedom – Peace – Equality**, in this order and with visual separation.

### 25. Federation membership

`சம்மேளனம் காங்கிரஸ் சார்புடையது எனக்கருதி` is more specific than “Congress-oriented.”

**T2 revision:** **Congress students too joined, believing the Federation to be Congress-affiliated.**

`பொதுமாணவர்கள்` is clarified by the source immediately as students belonging to no party.

**T2 revision:** **Of those two hundred, one hundred were unaffiliated students who belonged to no party.**

### 26. Closing current / depth image

T1 mixes “course” with “sweep,” weakening the linked water imagery.

Tamil:
> `சம்மேளனத்தின் போக்கு நம்மையும் சேர்த்து அடித்துச் செல்லும் போலிருந்தது. ஆழந் தெரியாமல் காலை விட்டு விட்டோம்...`

**T2 revision:** **The current of the Federation seemed ready to sweep us along with it. I kept worrying that we had put our foot in without knowing the depth.**

This preserves both physical images and lets the chapter end in Kalaignar's own apprehension rather than an explanatory conclusion.
