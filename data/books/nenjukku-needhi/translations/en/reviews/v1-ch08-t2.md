## Findings and revisions

### 1. Federation election cadence

T1:
> conducted elections for the Federation office-bearers

The Tamil is a straightforward election action. The plural English construction is heavier than necessary.

**T2 revision:** **held an election for the Federation office-bearers**.

### 2. Congress–Communist dominance and military movement

Tamil uses `ஆதிக்கம்` and then `படையெடுக்க`, so the political pressure is not merely neutral “influence.”

T1:
> Congress and Communist influence was gaining the upper hand ... That influence dared to make its advance through me

**T2 revision:** **Congress-Communist dominance was beginning to rise within the Federation. That dominance even dared to launch its advance through me.**

The military pressure of `படையெடுக்க` remains visible without forcing a literal “invade me.”

### 3. Membership-fee retrieval

T1:
> I urgently asked for it and took it.

This is stiff English for `அவசரமாகக் கேட்டு வாங்கினேன்`.

**T2 revision:** **I asked him for it at once and got it.**

The following twenty-five-rupee line is also clarified as money **returned**, not money “taken back.”

### 4. The seventy-five-rupee arithmetic

The P0 note securely establishes 100 rupees collected − 25 returned = 75 remaining.

T1:
> I enrolled in the new association all those to whom that money belonged.

**T2 revision:** **I enrolled in the new association all those whose fees made up that seventy-five rupees.**

This keeps the arithmetic causal without pretending to repair the anomalous Tamil form in the source repository.

### 5. 1941 inaugural event

`ஆரம்ப விழா` is an inaugural celebration/event rather than a bureaucratic “function” in ordinary English prose.

**T2 revision:** **the inaugural celebration of the Tamil Nadu Tamil Students' Association**.

The authored 1941/1942 sequence is untouched.

### 6. Bharathidasan's festival song

The source presents the poem as written specifically for the festival.

**T2 revision:** **Bharathidasan composed a song beginning...**

Verse lineation and the lion/enemy imagery remain intact.

### 7. Kumbakonam student conference

T1's “conference conducted by” is serviceable but stiff.

**T2 revision:** **the Dravidian Students' Association conference organized by Thavamani Rasan and Karunanandam**.

The historical present remains: the Tiruvarur festival **is taking place**, and the Kumbakonam conference **is beginning**.

### 8. Festival-finance escalation

T1 added “at the last minute” to the people suddenly brought in. The source says they were newly/suddenly brought, but does not need the extra temporal phrase.

**T2 revision:** remove **at the last minute**.

The donors' delay is also sharpened:

**T2 revision:** **they put me off for a week or ten days.**

This helps preserve the repeated pressure beats:

**no returned travel money → no fare for speakers → time running out → donors delay → no way out → steal.**

### 9. Gold chain: preserve where it is worn

The source repeatedly places the gold chain on `கை`; P0 protects the material detail against automatic conversion into a necklace.

T1:
> wear a gold chain on my hand

**T2 revision:** **wear a gold chain around my wrist**.

The later sentence is revised consistently to **wearing a chain around my wrist**.

This is English bodily clarity, not a change of object.

### 10. Cat-like entry into the room

T1:
> Like a cat along the verandah, I slipped into the room

**T2 revision:** **I crept along the verandah and into the room like a cat.**

The concrete image survives while the English movement becomes natural.

### 11. Mother's suffering as an internal question

The source punctuation is damaged, but the sequence is secure and P0 authorizes reconstruction of the internal dialogue.

T1:
> How much Mother will suffer when she finds the gold chain missing!

**T2 revision:** **What will Mother go through when she finds the gold chain missing?**

The next question about whether the family can simply replace the chain remains separate.

### 12. The open box

`என்ன ஆச்சரியம்!` is immediate surprise in the theft scene.

T1:
> What a wonder!

**T2 revision:** **What a surprise!**

No extra moral commentary is added before the blunt source sentence: **I stole it.**

### 13. Chettiar Bank and the speakers' fare

T1's **Tiruvarur Chettiar Bank** risks sounding like a securely established formal English institution name.

**T2 revision:** **the Chettiar Bank in Tiruvarur**.

This keeps the source institution concrete while leaving exact proper-name normalization for the terminology gate.

T1's **sent the speakers home without stint** is also too literal.

**T2 revision:** **sent the speakers back to their towns with ample fare**.

### 14. The uproar at home

The source has plural `படுபாவிகள்`.

**T2 revision:** **Who knows what wretched sinners took it!**

The close of the scene is also made less mechanically literal:

**T2 revision:** **Such words came from Mother's trembling voice.**

Her curse remains exactly the thing that triggers Kalaignar's comic reasoning about everyone eventually turning to dust.

### 15. Magic and divination

`மந்திரம், ஆரூடம் எல்லாம் கேட்டும்` is active consultation, not merely passive “spells being tried.”

**T2 revision:** **We even consulted magic and divination, but nothing helped.**

### 16. Mounting interest on the pledged chain

T1's “swallowed up entirely” makes the financial mechanism obscure.

**T2 revision:** **The interest ... mounted until the chain was on the verge of being lost altogether.**

The subsequent sale, debt payment, leftover four or five rupees, hotel meal and cinema remain intact. Kalaignar's self-indicting humour depends on retaining all of those material details.

### 17. Mother's colloquial affection and later approval

T1:
> Why should my boy's hand be bare?

The Tamil `எங்கய்யாவுக்கு வெறுங்கையா இருக்கே` is affectionate and colloquial, not a formal rhetorical question.

**T2 revision:** **My dear boy's wrist is bare!**

After the later confession, T1's “praise” is slightly too formal for `பாராட்டு` in this family scene.

**T2 revision:** **The approval rising in her heart showed itself on her face, and I understood that too.**

The line **You mischievous boy!** remains.

### 18. Public-meeting organizers who disappear

`தங்கள் தங்கள் பிரதாபங்களைக் காட்டிக் கொண்டவர்கள்` carries sarcasm toward people who display themselves on stage and then leave the practical burden behind.

T1:
> display their own glory

**T2 revision:** **show off their own importance**.

This keeps the sarcasm and naturally sets up the second theft.
