## Findings and revisions

### 1. English title — `தமிழ் காக்கும் போர் முனை`

The T1 title **The Battlefront to Save Tamil** preserves both the active martial image of `போர் முனை` and the protective force of `காக்கும்`. A flatter title such as “The Anti-Hindi Movement” would erase Kalaignar's chosen image.

**Action:** retain **The Battlefront to Save Tamil**.

### 2. `தமிழர் படை`

T1 repeatedly used generic **Tamil force**. The chapter is referring to the identifiable 1938 `தமிழர் படை` / Tamilar Padai march from Tiruchirappalli toward Madras. Historical English accounts commonly call it the **Tamil Brigade**, which preserves the organized marching body better than “force.”

**T2 revision:** use **Tamil Brigade** throughout this chapter.

This is historical-event naming, not a change to Kalaignar's description.

### 3. Pattukkottai Alagirisami and `அஞ்சா நெஞ்சன்`

The source first names **Pattukkottai Alagirisami** and later shortens him to Alagiri. T1 preserves that source alternation. English sources vary among Alagiri / Alagirisami / Azhagirisamy, so T2 does not impose a speculative global spelling beyond the source-led form.

The epithet `அஞ்சா நெஞ்சன்` is an active praise-name, not incidental metadata.

**Action:** retain **the “Fearless Heart”** and the full-name/short-name alternation; revisit only corpus-wide romanisation consistency at the terminology gate.

### 4. `வீரங்கொப்பளிக்கும் வரிகள்`

T1's **lines that bubbled with valour** is too mechanically literal and sounds comic in English. The Tamil image is active but expresses overflowing/brimming heroic force.

**T2 revision:** **the lines brimming with valour in Alagiri's speeches**.

This keeps the abundance image without changing its emotional register.

### 5. `வாட்ட சாட்டமான உருவம்` and `வணங்காமுடி மன்னன் போன்ற நடை`

T1's “broad, sturdy figure” is serviceable but weaker than the compact physical portrait. `வாட்ட சாட்டமான` describes a robust/strapping build.

**T2 revision:** **A strapping figure.**

The next fragment, **A gait like a king whose head would never bow**, already preserves the `வணங்காமுடி` image and is retained.

### 6. The two-hundred-mile march

`இருநூறு கல் தொலைவு நடந்தே` explicitly says the brigade came **on foot**. T1's “all along the two hundred miles” loses the walking action.

**T2 revision:** **The Tamil Brigade walked the two hundred miles from Tiruchirappalli to Madras; anti-Hindi supporters all along the route held receptions for it.**

The historical distance sense of `கல்` remains controlled by the P0 note and by the chapter's later `இருநூறு மைல்`.

The closing action `வீர நடை போட்டது` is also concrete.

**T2 revision:** **marched with a heroic stride toward Madras** rather than the flatter “marched heroically.”

### 7. Alagiri's three-beat oratorical crescendo

The page-level witness gives three separate exclamations:

- `புலி உறுமியது!`
- `சிம்மம் கர்ச்சித்தது!`
- `கோடை இடி குமுறியது!`

T1 blurred the distinct verbs.

**T2 revision:** **The tiger growled! The lion roared! Summer thunder rumbled!**

The fragments and escalation remain separate.

### 8. `தமிழுக்கும் தமிழனுக்கும்`

T1's “Tamil and the Tamil” is source-conscious but unnecessarily opaque here. The singular `தமிழன்` functions collectively.

**T2 revision:** **Tamil and the Tamil people**.

This keeps the language/people pairing while avoiding an English phrase likely to be misread as a grammatical error.

### 9. Student procession song

The source says the students together `ஒலிப்பர்` the song. T1's “would raise it together” is vague.

**T2 revision:** **all the students would sing it together**.

The first refrain line is also made idiomatic without reducing its imperative force:

**T2 revision:** **“Come, everyone, let us go to war!”**

The second line retains the source's `இந்திப் பேய்` as **Hindi demon**, and the refrain remains two lines.

### 10. Jagadeesan anecdote

The green-stone-ring sentence carries the reportative `தெரிவித்தாராம்`. T1 correctly says **It is said that...** and does not elevate the anecdote into verified fact. The source says only `பச்சைக்கல்`; T2 does not invent “emerald.”

**Action:** retain.

### 11. `வெறி / பற்று` rhetorical turn

The argument is not merely about an abstract “attachment”; Kalaignar is defending intense devotion to Tamil against the charge of `வெறி`.

**T2 revision:** **Some may call that feeling frenzy. If devotion is to be called frenzy, then that meaning is perfectly right!**

The repeated term remains audible rather than being paraphrased as “enthusiasm.”

### 12. N. V. Natarajan and Maraimalai Adigal sentence

The page-48/49 name join is secure as **N. V. Natarajan**; contemporary English evidence also supports the spelling **Peddunaickenpet**.

T1's “because of the abundance of their love for Tamil” is wooden.

**T2 revision:** **Was it not because of their great love for Tamil that...**

The rhetorical question remains a rhetorical question.

### 13. Classroom command and `பலகை`

The teacher's `ம்! படி பார்க்கலாம்?` is a challenge, not a genuine information question.

**T2 revision:** **“Hm! Let's see you read them!”**

Later, after the slap, `பலகையில் உட்கார்ந்து விட்டேன்` cannot mean that Kalaignar sat on the blackboard. Tamil lexicon usage permits `பலகை` as a board used as a seat/bench, and the classroom action securely requires a seat.

**T2 revision:** **I steadied myself and sat down on the bench.**

This is contextual lexical resolution, not source correction.

### 14. Expected retaliation and `மறியல் அறப்போர்`

The source says teachers expected him to organize `வேலை நிறுத்தம் போன்ற கிளர்ச்சிகள்`—agitations such as a strike. T1's “some agitation, perhaps a strike” weakens the construction.

**T2 revision:** **launch agitations such as a strike against the Hindi teacher**.

At the school gate, `மறியல் அறப்போர்` combines picketing with `அறப்போர்`, a non-violent/moral struggle term.

**T2 revision:** **activists of the Tamil movement carried on daily picketing as a non-violent struggle.**

Do not reduce this simply to “protest.”

### 15. Thirty-six-year retrospective — `பீடு நடை`

The relative-time shift is correct in T1 and must remain relative rather than being silently converted into a calendar year.

Lexically, `பீடு நடை` is a bold/majestic gait.

**T2 revision:** **a majestic stride and a proud gaze come to me from somewhere!**

The paired `மூண்டெழுந்த / முளைத்தெழுந்தவன்` flare/sprout imagery is retained.

### 16. Thalamuthu and Natarajan — `தமிழ்க் காளையர்`

T1 retains the concrete bull metaphor rather than replacing the two men with abstract “martyrs.”

**Action:** retain **the two Tamil bulls Thalamuthu and Natarajan became corpses in prison** at T2. Its deliberate bluntness is a T3 English-read question, not a reason to erase the Tamil image now.

### 17. Anna quotation — `இன உணர்ச்சி` and `மணிகள்`

The quotation's cumulative structure is intact: collective feeling wells up; sacrifice becomes known; truth emerges; labour bears fruit; mothers are seen ready for prison; and the light of life-giving `மணிகள்` becomes visible.

T1's **our sense of race** is intentionally close to the historical political register of `இன உணர்ச்சி`, but the exact corpus-wide English for `இனம்` remains context-dependent.

**Action:** retain **sense of race** for this T2; explicitly revisit `இன உணர்ச்சி` at the terminology/cultural gate.

Retain **the light of gems like Thalamuthu and Natarajan** for `மணிகளின் ஒளி`.

### 18. All-India political widening

T1 preserves the shift from the narrator's school memory to Hindu-Muslim conflict, Bose in East Bengal, Jinnah, the Muslim League and compulsory Hindi.

Two English refinements improve accuracy without altering the political claim:

- **opposition from Muslims** instead of “opposition of Muslim people”;
- `காந்தியார்` → **Gandhiji** to retain the local honorific register already used elsewhere in this translation.

**T2 revisions:** apply both.

### 19. Final Muslim League / flag image

T1's “marched in the ranks of the anti-Hindi opponents” is clumsy.

**T2 revision:** **Muslim League leaders in Tamil Nadu too marched in the anti-Hindi ranks.**

The final coordinated image remains:

**the Tamil flag and the green crescent flag flying together.**

The source's full stop between `தமிழ்க் கொடியும்.` and `பச்சைப் பிறைக் கொடியும்` is treated as disruptive punctuation, not as a command to separate the two coordinated images.
