## Governing question

Does the English reproduce the chapter's intellectual and emotional route, rather than merely preserving isolated facts and sentences?

## Chapter architecture

### 1. Orphaned beginning

Ayyathurai and Periya Nayagathammal anticipate parenthood → the child is born → mother dies within days → father dies soon after → the infant's immediate problem is hunger.

**English:** preserved.

### 2. Miracle rejected; human affection answers

Kalaignar invokes the story of Parvati feeding Thirugnana Sambandar only to say that such a miracle is story, not ordinary reality → Thillaiyammal and Meenakshi become the practical human answer.

**English:** preserved. The rationalist turn is not separated from the tenderness that follows it.

### 3. Naming and personal revelation

Pearl/shell wordplay leads to the name Muthuvel → Kalaignar pauses and reveals that the orphaned child was his father.

**English:** preserved, including the sigh and direct reader-address.

### 4. Father as many-sided village figure

Farmer → vidwan → poet → Sanskrit learning → storyteller → composer of folk satire.

**English:** preserved without reducing Muthuvelar to a single later ideological identity.

### 5. Courage demonstrated through song

First, a song becomes the voice of poor villagers against the powerful leaseholder → then a much riskier `ஏலேலோ` satire attacks sexual hypocrisy and powerful people, including relatives.

**English:** preserved. The songs remain evidence in Kalaignar's argument about his father's courage rather than decorative quotations.

The two unresolved expressions inside the second song do not obscure this structural function.

### 6. Son judges himself through father

After displaying the father's audacity, Kalaignar turns the comparison inward: he openly admits that he himself lacks his father's courage.

**English:** preserved as the argumentative payoff of the song sequence.

### 7. Apparent contradiction: fearless critic and intense believer

The narrative pivots sharply: this same fearless satirist is a deeply religious man, a manthirikar, healer and devotee → medicine and mantra are held together with Kalaignar's amused ambiguity.

**English:** preserved. The translation does not simplify the father into either rationalist precursor or credulous believer.

### 8. Marriage, bereavement and private devotion

First marriage → death of Kunjammal → extinguished lineage-lamp becomes an inextinguishable inner flame and family deity → second marriage and second bereavement → Anjugam enters.

**English:** preserved, including the lamp/flame image.

### 9. Mother as emotional centre; household as lived world

Kalaignar's extended praise of Anjugam → parents work the fields → household tools, fishing hooks and coconut-picking poles concretise family life → coconut-on-head anecdote releases the passage into humour.

**English:** preserved. Agricultural detail has not been compressed as incidental background.

### 10. Childlessness transformed through literature

Longing for children → Tirukkural on children's hands turning gruel sweeter than nectar → Kalaignar expands the couplet into an imagined domestic scene → daughters are born but parents continue longing for a son.

**English:** preserved. The Kural and Kalaignar's prose interpretation remain causally connected.

### 11. Calendrical accumulation → Kalaignar's birth

Multiple era/calendrical systems and auspicious phrasing create ceremonial expectation → abrupt reveal: `That child was I.`

**English:** preserved.

### 12. Birth immediately becomes comic family memory

Infant Kalaignar → burglary → mother's thali stolen though husband is alive → retrospective joke that crying might have spared him later troubles → watchdog barks only after thieves leave → father ties replacement thali → `four thalis for three wives` → father's philosophy that the thief spared no gold because gold carries no sin.

**English:** preserved. The chapter ends not in solemn autobiography but in inherited family humour that characterises both father and son.
