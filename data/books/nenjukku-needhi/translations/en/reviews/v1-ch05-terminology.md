## Governing question

Does the frozen English use historically appropriate institutional English, stable proper names, culturally meaningful honorifics, movement vocabulary and recurring political language without either modernising Kalaignar's register or creating false exoticism?

## Findings

### 1. `முனுசாமி நாயுடு`

The chapter had `Munusami Naidu`. Historical English references consistently use **Munuswamy Naidu** for the Justice Party leader and First Minister who lost the party leadership to the Raja of Bobbili in 1932.

**Revision:** `Munusami Naidu` → **Munuswamy Naidu**.

This is a proper-name normalisation for traceability, not an alteration of Kalaignar's account.

### 2. Justice Party / Swaraj Party / Indian National Congress

These remain established English political proper names. No alternation with literal or transliterated forms is needed.

**Action:** retain **Justice Party**, **Swaraj Party**, and **Indian National Congress**.

### 3. Madras Presidency office vocabulary

The chapter crosses constitutional periods. The existing T2 distinction remains correct:

- Raja of Bobbili in 1932 — **First Minister**;
- Dr P. Subbarayan's 1926–1930 government — **First Minister**;
- Rajaji in 1937 — **Premier of the Madras Presidency**;
- Dr Subbarayan in 1938 — **Education Minister**.

**Action:** retain. Do not modernise any of these to `Chief Minister`.

### 4. Panneerselvam / Khalifullah / Natarajan

The T2-normalised forms **Sir A. T. Panneerselvam**, **Khan Bahadur Khalifullah Sahib**, and **Natarajan** remain the chapter's controlled English forms.

**Action:** retain.

### 5. Political and cultural honorifics

Kalaignar uses titles and epithets as part of political memory rather than detachable metadata. The following are therefore intentionally retained in culturally visible form:

- **Navalar Somasundara Bharathiar**;
- **Arignar Anna**;
- **Perarignar Anna**;
- **Muthamizh Kaavalar K. A. P. Viswanatham**;
- **Moovalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar**;
- **Tamilavel Uma Maheswaran Pillai**;
- **Revolutionary Poet Bharathidasan**.

The mix of transliterated and translated epithets is deliberate: transparent English epithets may be translated where they remain natural and recognisable, while culturally embedded titles remain visible rather than being stripped away.

### 6. `காந்தியடிகள்`

The glossary permits contextual **Gandhiji / Gandhi**, but this chapter's linked 1938 passage begins with the reverential `காந்தி அடிகளின்` and continues with the same referent. Switching from `Gandhiji` to bare `Gandhi` inside the immediate comparison weakens that local register.

**Revision:** `Gandhi's welfare, fame, service or honour` → **`Gandhiji's welfare, fame, service or honour`**.

This keeps the honorific register consistent within the passage without forcing `Gandhiji` mechanically across the whole corpus.

### 7. `வடமொழி`

In Maraimalai Adigal's lament, the established lexical sense is **Sanskrit**. T2's correction from literal `northern language` remains appropriate.

**Action:** retain **Sanskrit**.

### 8. `இறும்பூது`

The literary sense is **wonder / amazement**. The current `I am filled with wonder` avoids adding pride not present in the word itself.

**Action:** retain.

### 9. `தமிழர் தலைவர்`

The exact phrase functions first as Periyar's public epithet and then as the title of Sami Chidambaranar's biography. The chapter consistently uses **Leader of the Tamils** in both places.

**Action:** retain the lexical echo.

### 10. `தமிழன்` as collective personification

The line after Thalamuthu and Natarajan's deaths personifies `தமிழன்` as the collective Tamil who dips the language-struggle flag in the heroes' blood and sends it skyward. The unusual English **The Tamil** was deliberately retained at T3 because converting it to a neutral plural would weaken the singular collective figure.

**Action:** retain **The Tamil** as a source-driven rhetorical personification.

### 11. Language-war vocabulary

The chapter alternates agitation, struggle, battle and war in ways driven by the Tamil (`கிளர்ச்சி`, `போராட்டம்`, `போர்`, `போர்க்களம்`). These are not synonyms to be mechanically standardised.

**Action:** retain the existing contextual distribution, including **anti-Hindi agitation**, **anti-Hindi struggle**, **anti-Hindi war**, **language struggle**, and **battlefield in the language struggle**.

### 12. `இனத்தின் பெருமை`

The phrase appears in Kalaignar's 1938 catalogue of challenged political and cultural values. The current **the pride of race** preserves the historical collective register of `இனம்` without silently replacing it with a later administrative or identity category.

**Action:** retain for this chapter, but do **not** freeze `இனம்` globally. Later occurrences of `இனம்`, `இன உணர்வு` and `தமிழினம்` remain context-dependent under the glossary.

### 13. `சிந்து`

The closing audience chorus calls Anna's speech a `கொஞ்சு தமிழ்ச் சிந்து`. `சிந்து` names a Tamil song/poetic form rather than merely a generic song. The current **a lilting Tamil chindu** retains the cultural form while `lilting` carries the affectionate musical quality of `கொஞ்சு` without over-explaining it.

**Action:** retain **Tamil chindu** and add a glossary note that `chindu` may be preserved when the genre/form matters.

### 14. Anna's public chorus and translated epithets

`Gold of Tanjore`, `Lion of Tamil`, pomegranate pearls, the opened sluice, Courtallam waterfall and the audience's fragmentary exclamations are rhetorical/cultural content, not terminology noise.

**Action:** retain. Do not flatten the closing chorus into neutral prose or remove its public-speaking texture.

### 15. Historical claims versus terminology

The chapter's explicit **1936** election statement remains Kalaignar's authored chronology even though modern historical summaries often date the decisive provincial election differently. Terminology review does not become fact-correction.

**Action:** retain the authored chronology exactly as already controlled in source notes.
