## Method

The revised English chapter was read as a continuous English literary work, without using the Tamil to supply missing sense during the read. The purpose was to test readability, rhythm, tonal movement and narrative continuity after T2.

The two explicitly unresolved song expressions were not treated as ordinary copy-editing problems. They remain visibly provisional because smoothing them into fluent English would manufacture semantic certainty that the available source evidence does not support.

## English-read findings

### Opening bereavement sequence — PASS

The chapter moves clearly from Ayyathurai and Periya Nayagathammal's anticipation of a child to the mother's death, the father's death and the helpless infant. The short sentence `Who was to relieve that hunger?` preserves the abrupt human problem before Kalaignar's turn against the miraculous feeding story.

### Foster care and naming — PASS

The pearl/shell image remains intelligible and leads naturally to the revelation `That child was my father.` The deep-sigh framing is retained rather than converted to neutral biography.

### Muthuvelar portrait and first protest song — PASS

The sequence from farmer/vidwan/poet to fearless village satirist is readable. The first song works as a compact collective protest and remains visibly verse.

### Long `ஏலேலோ` satire — PASS FOR READABILITY / SOURCE HOLD RETAINED

The framing prose makes the narrative purpose of the quoted song clear. `போகசாலை` and `ஊடல்`, corrected at T2, now read coherently in English.

The provisional `taragu-mootai` and the conservative sari-end line interrupt smooth English, but that interruption is intentional and editorially preferable to an invented translation. Translation notes identify the reason. These two lines therefore do not fail T3; they remain a final-approval source/interpretation hold.

### Courage → devotion reversal — PASS

`For all that, my father was a devotee of God through and through!` successfully preserves the surprising turn from fearless social criticism to intense religiosity. The mantra/medicine passage retains Kalaignar's amused ambiguity rather than explaining away the contradiction.

### Marriage, loss and Anjugam — PASS

The Kunjammal lamp/flame/family-deity imagery remains emotionally legible. The long Anjugam sentence is deliberately expansive; it reads as an outpouring of affection rather than as a biographical list and should not be shortened for conventional English economy.

### Agricultural household and coconut anecdote — PASS

The domestic inventory and fishing/coconut details slow the narrative in a purposeful way, giving the father a lived village texture. The paired coconut/head exclamations land as the intended comic release.

### Childlessness, Kural and birth — PASS

The Kural is kept as verse and the following gruel-to-nectar explanation makes Kalaignar's emotional use of it clear. The calendrical accumulation before `That child was I.` retains its ceremonial build and abrupt payoff.

### Burglary and thali sequence — PASS

The dramatic present, fragments and family dialogue read naturally enough to preserve the oral storytelling effect. The sequence escalates through the cradle-cloth joke, the delayed watchdog, the four-thalis-for-three-wives line and the father's `no sin in gold` explanation without editorially explaining the humour.
