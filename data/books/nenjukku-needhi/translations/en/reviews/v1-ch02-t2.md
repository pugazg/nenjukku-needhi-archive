## Source-review findings

### 1. `பார் போக்குவது?`

The page-level witness `data/books/nenjukku-needhi/cleaned/volume1/0022.md` reproduces the same extracted form `பார் போக்குவது?`; it therefore does not resolve the anomalous token. Context remains unambiguous: Kalaignar is asking who will relieve the orphaned infant's hunger and how.

**Action:** retain the contextual English question and keep the intervention disclosed in source notes. Do not alter the read-only source.

### 2. `அவ்ளோ`

Page 24 contains `ஆனால் அவ்ளோ, கள்ளக் காதலர்களில் ஒருவனோடு...`. In syntax and narrative context the referent is the woman just described; the intended reading functions as `அவளோ`.

**Action:** translate the feminine contrast ('yet she...') and record the extraction form explicitly.

### 3. Long `ஏலேலோ` song — page-level witness

The archive's independent page-level witness `0024.md` reproduces the disputed forms exactly, including:

- `தன் வீட்டுத் தரகுமூட்டை / தலைமேலிருந்தும்`
- `தனியாகப் போகசாலை / மனைக் கட்டிடங்களிருந்தும்`
- `அவசரமாய் முந்தானையால் / கூட்டியே நின்று`

This witness proves that the chapter JSON did not itself create these spellings, but it does not by itself establish the meaning of every compound.

### 4. `போகசாலை`

A Tamil Digital Library lexical/contextual witness (TVA_BOK_0012940, p. 50) explicitly gives `போகசாலை (சயன சாலை)`, establishing the term as a sleeping hall/bedchamber in architectural usage.

**T2 correction:** T1's `pleasure-house` was over-interpretive. Replace it with a bedchamber/sleeping-quarters rendering.

### 5. `ஊடியும்`

Tamil lexical sources define `ஊடல்` in the lovers' context as a small love-quarrel / feigned sulking or anger between lovers. Kalaignar's own later explanation of the term elsewhere likewise treats it as the lovers' playful quarrel before reunion.

**T2 correction:** T1's `dallied` loses the specific erotic-literary sense. Render the action as a lovers' quarrel followed by joining/reunion.

### 6. `அவன்பால் விழுந்த காமுகர்கள்`

The page-level witness `0025.md` also reads `அவன்பால்`, although the immediately preceding and following context identifies the female heroine as the person attracting the lovers. T1 silently rendered the contextual referent as `her`.

**Action:** keep `her` in English but disclose the source-form/gender mismatch in source notes.

### 7. Remaining unresolved lexical area

Two parts of the long song remain insufficiently secure from the available text witnesses:

- the compound `தரகுமூட்டை`;
- the exact syntactic force/object relationship in `முந்தானையால் கூட்டியே நின்று`.

**Action:** remove T1's confident paraphrase of `தரகுமூட்டை`; retain the Tamil compound transliterated in the provisional English line. Render the `முந்தானை` line conservatively without inventing an unexpressed object. Chapter approval remains blocked until a stronger source/image witness resolves these readings or an editorial decision explicitly accepts transliteration as the final treatment.

## Kalaignar-voice findings and T2 actions

### 8. `பெரிய கோவில்கள், குட்டி தேவதைகள்`

The big/little contrast has a lightly amused oral quality.

**Action:** preserve it as `big temples and little deities`, rather than smoothing both into generic religious sites.

### 9. `பச்சைச் சிசு`

T1's `green little infant` is mechanically literal. In context `பச்சை` conveys extreme newness/tender helplessness.

**Action:** use `tender little infant`.

### 10. Miracle-story pivot

`...கதைதானே தவிர, நடைமுறை அல்ல` is a sharp, almost conversational rationalist turn after the infant's hunger.

**Action:** keep it direct: the divine feeding is a story, not how ordinary life works. Do not academicise the contrast.

### 11. `மங்கையர் திலகங்கள்`

T1's `two noble women` loses the ornamental praise in `திலகம்`.

**Action:** restore some elevation without awkward literalism: `two women, jewels among women`.

### 12. First protest song

T1's `what are we poor to do?` is unnatural English and weakens the collective voice.

**Action:** `what are we poor people to do?` while retaining `spears` / `lances` and the escalating defiance.

### 13. Moral narration

T1 added `accepted` in `strayed from the accepted path`; the Tamil simply says `நெறி தவறிவிட்டாள்`.

**Action:** remove the translator's qualifier and keep Kalaignar's own moral framing.

### 14. `பழுத்த கடவுள் பக்தர்`

`thoroughgoing devotee` is accurate but stiff. The Tamil is emphatic and slightly conversational, especially after the social satire.

**Action:** use `a devotee of God through and through!`.

### 15. Anjugam praise passage

T1's `with our whole mouths` is too literal for `வாய் நிறைய`. The passage must remain expansive and affectionate, but sound like literary English.

**Action:** preserve the fullness image as `with the word filling our mouths` and keep the long accumulating structure rather than reducing it to a biographical summary.

### 16. Coconut punchline

The paired exclamations are deliberate comic timing.

**Action:** preserve the two-beat joke: neither the coconut nor Appa's head was harmed.

### 17. Birth register — `அவதரித்தது`

After the grand calendrical enumeration, Kalaignar uses elevated `அவதரித்தது` and then abruptly reveals `அந்தக் குழந்தைதான் நான்`.

**Action:** restore some ceremonial elevation (`a child made its auspicious advent`) so that `That child was I.` retains its payoff.

### 18. Burglary scene — dramatic present and fragments

Kalaignar shifts into a vivid present (`தூங்கிக் கொண்டு, இருக்கிறேன்`) and uses fragments such as `பண்டம் பாத்திரங்கள். ஒன்று விடாமல்...` before the jokes begin.

**Action:** preserve the scene-setting present and some fragmentary oral rhythm; do not smooth the whole burglary into conventional retrospective narration.

### 19. Family dialogue

T1's `It was fortunate that you did not cry` is more formal than the mother's `நல்ல வேளை`.

**Action:** use a more immediate family voice: `Good thing you didn't cry...`.
