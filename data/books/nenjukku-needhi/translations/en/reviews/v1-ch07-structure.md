## Gate result

**PASS.**

The English preserves the chapter's distinctive architecture. Kalaignar first builds a broad political field—Congress and League, Gandhi and Jinnah, Tripuri, Bose, Nehru, Europe, China, M. N. Roy, Forward Bloc and Communist organizing. That material is not detachable background: the Communist preparations become the explicit bridge to the man who seeks out Karunanidhi.

The chapter then changes scale abruptly and deliberately. A national political current reaches a thin fifteen-year-old schoolboy because he runs a handwritten journal. From there writing practice becomes publishing labour, the burden of fifty hand-copied issues produces `Murasoli`, and the same young writer is recruited into student organization. The closing current/depth image therefore gathers the whole chapter: large political currents have finally reached the young narrator, and he is conscious that he has stepped into something whose depth he does not yet know.

The result remains autobiography conducted through political history, not political history followed by an unrelated childhood anecdote.
