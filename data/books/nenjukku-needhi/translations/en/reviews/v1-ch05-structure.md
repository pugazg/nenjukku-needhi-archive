## Governing question

Does the English preserve Kalaignar's movement from Justice Party institutional history into his own political awakening, through the anti-Hindi struggle and the crises of 1938, and finally into the public emergence of Periyar and Anna—without flattening the chapter into detached chronology?

## Structural map

### 1. Justice Party leadership changes under pressure

The chapter opens in 1932 with the no-confidence resolution against Munusami Naidu and the Tanjore conference at which the Raja of Bobbili becomes party leader and First Minister.

Function: begin not with abstract decline but with an institutional rupture and transfer of authority.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 2. The ministry's intended life stretches beyond its term

The Simon Commission report is delayed, extending the ministry's life from three years to seven; Muthiah Chettiar and Sir A. T. Panneerselvam appear among the intervening ministers.

Function: establish a government surviving through postponement while the party's leadership configuration changes.

English: **PRESERVED**, including the `life of the ministry` metaphor.

### 3. Party collapse immediately returns history to the schoolboy narrator

Kalaignar states that the Justice Party collapsed in the 1936 election and, in the same movement, recalls himself in Fifth Standard, mastering the history of the Raja of Panagal and winning his teacher's praise.

Function: prevent the political chronology from becoming impersonal; public history is explicitly tied back to the child's political education.

English: **PRESERVED**. The source's authored 1936 chronology remains unchanged.

### 4. Congress enters government and the Swaraj Party disappears

The 1937 Congress ministry assumes office; direct Congress participation in the election is linked to the disappearance of the Swaraj Party, with Rajaji as Premier.

Function: mark a constitutional and party-political transition that sets up the compulsory-Hindi conflict.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 5. Compulsory Hindi turns government policy into mass agitation

Rajaji makes Hindi compulsory; Tamil lovers and scholars rise, organise agitations, pass resolutions and pour condemnations through newspapers until anti-Hindi agitation fills cities, hamlets and villages.

Function: widen a government decision into a society-wide movement and accelerate the chapter's oral cadence.

English: **PRESERVED WITHOUT COMPRESSION**.

### 6. Opposition hardens and cracks appear inside Congress

Rajaji responds by imposing Hindi more forcefully. Kalaignar then notes that even Tamils inside Congress did not favour compulsory Hindi, with Navalar Somasundara Bharathiar leaving Congress and roaring against Hindi.

Function: show that resistance is not merely external opposition but also exposes internal political unease.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 7. Rajaji's numerical claim is reversed by Panneerselvam

Rajaji says only two people oppose Hindi. Panneerselvam immediately replies that only one person is bringing Hindi in, so the majority must be on the side of opposition.

Function: compress the political argument into a memorable arithmetic punchline and public victory.

English: **PRESERVED AS DIRECT DIALOGUE**.

### 8. Maraimalai Adigal gives the language struggle a lamenting civilisational voice

Maraimalai Adigal laments that Sanskrit and English have already weakened Tamil and that Hindi would bring destruction; the anti-Hindi movement then becomes a source of consolation to him.

Function: deepen the conflict from policy dispute into an emotional language-survival argument.

English: **PRESERVED**, including the lament and consolation turn.

### 9. June 3 binds the movement directly to Kalaignar's own birth date

The 3 June 1938 Saidapet conference under Maraimalai Adigal is linked to Kalaignar's own June 3 birthday, producing repeated reflection and wonder.

Function: fuse public mobilisation with autobiographical time; the movement's history becomes personally charged without leaving the historical narrative.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 10. Periyar becomes commander of a collective anti-Hindi front

Periyar leads the 1938 anti-Hindi war; Anna, K. A. P. Viswanatham, Somasundara Bharathiar, Moovalur Ramamirtham Ammaiyar, poets and Tamil scholars stand in the front ranks.

Function: present the movement as organised collective struggle rather than a single-person episode.

English: **PRESERVED WITHOUT NAME COMPRESSION**.

### 11. Mass imprisonment culminates in death and the blood-dipped flag

School pickets lead more than a thousand men and women to prison. Thalamuthu and Natarajan die in prison, and Kalaignar turns their blood into the image of the Tamil's language-struggle flag flying until it touches the sky.

Function: escalate from protest to sacrifice and convert death into a political image of defiant continuation.

English: **PRESERVED WITHOUT SOFTENING**.

### 12. Fourteen years become the hinge from the boy's age to national history

Kalaignar pauses: fourteen years separate 1924 and 1938; he has reached Second Form; how many events have taken place in the country!

Function: reset the scale through his own age before widening outward into Indian and world history.

English: **PRESERVED**, including the repeated `How many, how many` force.

### 13. Bose and Nehru anchor the 1938 all-India frame

Subhas Chandra Bose is elected president of the Indian National Congress; Nehru leaves for Europe and is absent from India for the year.

Function: establish 1938 as a politically charged national moment before returning to Tamil Nadu's Hindi conflict.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 14. The compulsory-Hindi decision is located inside Rajaji's ministry

Dr Subbarayan's earlier Justice-supported government is recalled, then his 1938 role as Education Minister is named; Kalaignar locates the compulsory-Hindi decision in his room on 25 February.

Function: tie the mass agitation back to a precise governmental decision and a known political figure.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 15. Kanchipuram turns decision into organised public resistance

Two days later, Khalifullah Sahib raises the anti-Hindi flag at Kanchipuram; Somasundara Bharathiar speaks against Hindi and Periyar unveils Bharathidasan's portrait.

Function: move rapidly from administrative decision to symbolic and public mobilisation.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 16. The budget resolution introduces Anna through a delayed reveal

A resolution opposes spending public money on unnecessary Hindi; Tamilavel Uma Maheswaran Pillai and N. Dandapani support it. Kalaignar then asks who moved it and answers: Perarignar Anna—then C. N. Annadurai, M.A.

Function: bring Anna into the political history first through action, then through name and later stature.

English: **PRESERVED**, including the reader-address question and before-fame reveal.

### 17. Tamil Nadu's language battlefield opens into a world-crisis catalogue

Kalaignar links 1938 to Kemal Pasha's death, Hitler's swallowing of Czechoslovakia, the approach of the Second World War, Stalin's purge and the conflict between Gandhian non-violence and Bose's politics.

Function: place the Tamil language struggle inside a simultaneous world of ideological and political upheaval.

English: **PRESERVED WITHOUT ENCYCLOPEDIC REORDERING**.

### 18. The Voltaire quotation becomes a lens for the Bose–Gandhi relationship

Kalaignar gives his Tamil-mediated Voltaire quotation about defending another's right to speak, then uses it to describe Bose disagreeing with Gandhi while fiercely opposing anything that harmed Gandhi's welfare, fame, service or honour.

Function: shift from events to a principle about disagreement, loyalty and political conduct.

English: **PRESERVED**, with Kalaignar's quotation retained rather than replaced by canonical external wording.

### 19. 1938 is summed up as a challenge to political and cultural values

Non-violence, democracy, individual rights, self-government, secure frontiers, racial/national pride, the mother tongue and honest politics are all said to have come under severe challenge across the world.

Function: convert the preceding event catalogue into a thematic verdict on the age.

English: **PRESERVED AS AN ACCUMULATIVE LIST**.

### 20. Criticism of Periyar is voiced before his public elevation

Kalaignar reports accusations that Periyar opposed Hindi only as a weapon against Congress and cared neither for Tamil's development nor for Tamils.

Function: stage the hostile interpretation explicitly as reported speech before reversing toward public acceptance.

English: **PRESERVED AS REPORTED CRITICISM**, including the clipped `It was written. It was said.`

### 21. Periyar is accepted as `Leader of the Tamils`

The anti-Hindi leader comes to be accepted as the Leader of the Tamils, and Sami Chidambaranar's biography repeats that exact phrase in its title.

Function: move from contested motive to consolidated public identity, with a deliberate lexical echo between epithet and book title.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 22. Anna's name becomes sound, image and public reception

The name Annadurai begins to echo through every corner of Tamil Nadu. Kalaignar then yields the closing space to multiple audience voices praising Anna's speaking—music, restrained movement, pomegranate pearls, a sluice gate, firm arguments, undeniable statistics, Courtallam waterfall and a Tamil `chindu`.

Function: end not with retrospective biography but with Anna becoming audible in public memory; the chapter closes on reception itself as dramatic scene.

English: **PRESERVED AS A MULTI-VOICE CHORUS**.
