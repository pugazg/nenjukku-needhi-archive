## Minor English revisions

### 1. The peon cannot stop the boy

Current: `Even the peon at the doorway, pulling the punkah, cannot stop him.`

Revision: `Even the peon at the doorway, pulling the punkah, tries to stop him, but he pays no heed.`

Reason: the Tamil `தடுத்தும் கேட்கவில்லை` describes the boy ignoring the attempt to stop him, not merely the peon's inability. The revision is more idiomatic and restores the boy's active determination.

### 2. Kamalalayam in the headmaster's mind

Current: `In his mind turns the famous temple tank opposite—the Kamalalayam.`

Revision: `The famous temple tank opposite—the Kamalalayam—swirls through his mind.`

Reason: removes unnatural English word order while preserving the active image in `அவருடைய நினைவில் ... சுழன்றது`.

### 3. Brief hesitation

Current: `He thinks for a little while.`

Revision: `He thinks for a moment.`

Reason: more natural English with no change to the brief pause in `சிறிது யோசித்தார்`.

### 4. School result

Current: `with leading marks`

Revision: `with top marks`

Reason: idiomatic English for `முதன்மையான மார்க்குகள்`; no strengthening beyond the Tamil.

### 5. Party formation syntax

Current: `founded a political party under the name 'South Indian Liberal Federation.'`

Revision: `formed a political party called 'South Indian Liberal Federation.'`

Reason: removes bureaucratic translation syntax while preserving `ஓர் அரசியல் கட்சியைத் தோற்றுவித்தனர்` and the T2 proper-name normalisation.

### 6. Newspaper-to-party naming sentence

Current: `the party itself came to be known by the name of the English newspaper—the Justice Party.`

Revision: `the party itself came to be known by the English newspaper's name—the Justice Party.`

Reason: tighter natural English; the causal naming sequence remains unchanged.

### 7. `இந்தியா மந்திரி மாண்டேகு பிரபு`

Current: `the India Minister, Lord Montagu`

Revision: `the Secretary of State for India, Lord Montagu`

Reason: `India Minister` is not natural institutional English. The revision uses Montagu's historically attested official English office title while preserving Kalaignar's reference. This follows the same disclosed historical-institution normalisation policy already used at T2 for `First Minister` and `President of the Legislative Council`.

### 8. Remove translator-added hedging in the Home Rule comparison

Current: `More than the schemes of the Home Rule movement then in progress, it was the statement issued by the Justice Party that, in their view, revealed the true condition of Dravidian society.`

Revision: `More than the plans of the Home Rule movement then under way, it was the statement issued by the Justice Party that revealed the true condition of Dravidian society.`

Reason: `in their view` is not present in the Tamil and weakens Kalaignar's direct assertion. `plans` is the neutral equivalent of `திட்டங்கள்`; `schemes` risks unintended English pejoration.

### 9. Panagal Raja tenure sentence

Current: `the achievements secured for the Dravidians during the Raja of Panagal's period in office`

Revision: `the achievements secured for the Dravidians during the Raja of Panagal's tenure`

Reason: removes stiffness without altering `பனகல் அரசரின் ஆட்சிக் காலத்தில் ... சாதனைகள்`.

### 10. Justice Party after the three leaders' deaths

Current: `the Justice Party was left standing shaken.`

Revision: `the Justice Party stood shaken.`

Reason: restores natural English while retaining the compact physical image of `நீதிக்கட்சி கலகலத்து நின்றது` rather than replacing it with abstract political analysis.

## Deliberate features retained

- The opening remains in dramatic present and third person until `It was I myself!`; the boy is not identified early.
- The suicide threat remains blunt and immediate; it is not softened into indirect speech.
- `Five veli for the temple, five veli for the tank` remains a quoted local saying rather than being explanatory paraphrase.
- `political primer` remains the governing `அரிச்சுவடி` metaphor and the bridge from school memory to movement history.
- `great Dravidian community`, `removing its fetters`, `backward and depressed communities`, and related political vocabulary remain as established at T2.
- `First Minister` and `President of the Legislative Council` retain period institutional language.
- The repeated emphasis on `that statement` remains; T3 removes only the translator-added hedge, not Kalaignar's repetition.
- `morning star for the Dravidians`, `unending sleep in nature's lap`, `the country shed tears`, `flame of rationalism`, and `speed of a whirlwind` remain concrete metaphors rather than being converted into neutral historical prose.
- `weekly verbal debates` remains because `சொற்போர்` carries contest/oratorical energy beyond a generic `discussion`.
- Dates, election results, leaders and political evaluations remain translated as authored claims; T3 does not fact-correct the memoir.
