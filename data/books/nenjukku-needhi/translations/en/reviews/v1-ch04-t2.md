## Source and proper-name findings

### 1. `தென்னிந்திய நல உரிமைச் சங்கம்`

T1 rendered the Tamil name literally as `South Indian Welfare Rights Association`. The historically attested English proper name of the organization is **South Indian Liberal Federation (SILF)**; it was popularly known as the Justice Party after its English daily *Justice*. Multiple historical references and Tamil Nadu educational material use this English name.

**T2 action:** normalise the organization name in English to `South Indian Liberal Federation`. Record the choice as a proper-name normalisation, not a correction of Kalaignar's historical narrative.

### 2. `சட்டசயைத் தலைவர்` / `சட்டசபைத் தலைவர்`

T1 used `President of the Legislative Assembly`. For the 1920s Madras Presidency under dyarchy, the relevant institution was the **Madras Legislative Council**, whose presiding officer was the President of the Council. Contemporary office material also identifies the 1925 ministers and the Legislative Council as distinct institutional structures.

**T2 action:** render the office as `President of the Legislative Council` for both Sir P. Rajagopalachariar and Professor M. Ratnaswami. This is historical institutional normalisation, not a correction of Kalaignar's substantive claim.

### 3. `முதல் மந்திரி`

The phrase is retained as the period office title **First Minister**. Independent historical sources use `First Minister` for the 1920s Madras Presidency office, alongside later explanatory uses such as Premier/Chief Minister.

**T2 action:** keep `First Minister` rather than modernising the chapter to `Chief Minister`.

## Kalaignar-voice findings

### 4. `திராவிடப் பெருங்குடி`

T1's `great Dravidian community` is defensible and preserves Kalaignar's collective/social imagery. Do not reduce it to the modern bureaucratic category `non-Brahmins` merely because that is the political constituency most closely associated with the Justice Party.

**Action:** retain `great Dravidian community`.

### 5. `தாழ்த்தப்பட்ட மக்கள்` / `பின்னடைந்த, தாழ்த்தப்பட்ட சமுதாயங்கள்`

T1's `backward and depressed communities` preserves the historical social vocabulary without substituting a modern administrative label. Historical sources also use `Depressed Classes` for this period.

**Action:** retain `backward and depressed communities` and `people from the depressed communities` where Kalaignar uses the people-form.

### 6. `சுயமரியாதை இயக்கம்` and `பகுத்தறிவு`

The existing `Self-Respect Movement` and `rationalism` are the established English forms and fit Kalaignar's political register. They should not be weakened into `self-esteem movement` or `reasoning`.

**Action:** freeze these working forms for this chapter and add them to the living glossary.

### 7. `விடிவெள்ளி`

T1's `morning star of dawn` is redundant: `விடிவெள்ளி` itself is the morning star/Venus. The metaphor is political and should remain concrete.

**T2 action:** `who had shone as the morning star for the Dravidians`.

### 8. `சொற்போர்`

`சொற்போர்` is a standard Tamil rendering for debate/word-contest and carries a more active oral character than plain `discussion`.

**T2 action:** `weekly verbal debates` rather than flattening the activity to `weekly discussions`.

### 9. Political-primer architecture

The T1 correctly keeps the `அரிச்சுவடி` metaphor active: the Fifth Standard book becomes Kalaignar's political primer, after which the chapter expands into party formation, newspapers, representation, elections, ministries, labour confrontation, leadership deaths, Periyar, Self-Respect and Anna.

**Action:** retain the architecture; no compression or encyclopedia-style restructuring.
