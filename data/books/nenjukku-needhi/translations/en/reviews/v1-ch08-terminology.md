## Audit principles

This gate checks organization labels, personal and place names, movement-era vocabulary, cultural objects, family/ritual terms and institution wording against the living glossary, prior approved chapters and the source context.

Historical normalization is allowed only when secure. A plausible historical identity is not enough to override the memoir's wording or to turn a transparent working translation into a falsely precise formal English institutional name.

## Findings

### 1. Federation / Students' Federation

The chapter begins in direct continuity with the generic `மாணவர் சம்மேளனம்` established in `v1-ch07`.

The full glossary policy remains **Students' Federation**. Within `v1-ch08`, shortened **Federation** references are clear because the organization has already been established across the chapter boundary.

**Action:** retain **Federation** as contextual short form. Do not expand it to AISF or any other named organization.

### 2. `தமிழ் மாணவர் மன்றம்`

The body formed immediately after Kalaignar dissolves the Federation is a new organization and must stay distinct from it.

**Action:** retain **Tamil Students' Association** as the working chapter-level English form and chapter title.

The word **Association** is a transparent functional rendering of `மன்றம்`; this does not claim a separately attested official English corporate name.

### 3. `தமிழ்நாடு தமிழ் மாணவர் மன்றம்`

The memoir says the Tiruvarur body establishes contact with the Salem-based organization and then takes the wider name.

**Action:** retain **Tamil Nadu Tamil Students' Association**.

Do not shorten this to the local **Tamil Students' Association**, because the local-to-wider organizational movement is structurally important.

### 4. `திராவிடர் மாணவர் கழகம்`

The Kumbakonam conference body is currently rendered **Dravidian Students' Association**.

**Action:** retain as a transparent working English form.

The present source/control set does not securely establish a formal historical English institutional name, so the translation should not manufacture one.

### 5. Bharathidasan

The pinned chapter surface has anomalous `பாதிதாசனின்`, while P0 contextual evidence securely identifies the poet associated with the quoted festival lines as Bharathidasan.

**Action:** retain **Bharathidasan**.

This remains a disclosed source-sensitive normalization, not an unrecorded repair of the Tamil source.

### 6. Personal names

The chapter uses:

- **S. P. Chidambaram**
- **Venkataraman**
- **Thavamani Rasan**
- **Karunanandam**
- **Anbazhagan**

**Action:** retain the current source-led romanized forms.

The audit does not impose a corpus-wide romanization freeze for names that may receive stronger evidence later.

### 7. Place names

**Tiruvarur** and **Thanjavur** are stable working English place forms.

The source uses `குடந்தை`, a historical/alternate Tamil name for Kumbakonam; the English uses **Kumbakonam**.

**Action:** retain **Tiruvarur / Thanjavur / Kumbakonam**. The Kumbakonam normalization is geographic identification, not chronology correction.

### 8. `செட்டியார் பாங்கு`

T2 deliberately changed the phrase to **the Chettiar Bank in Tiruvarur**, rather than presenting **Tiruvarur Chettiar Bank** as though a formal corporate English name had been established.

**Action:** retain **the Chettiar Bank in Tiruvarur** as a cautious contextual rendering.

The bank/pledge function is secure. Exact corporate-name normalization remains reviewable if later source evidence appears.

### 9. `தோழர்`

The chapter calls Sanarthanam `தோழர்` in an overt movement/public-meeting setting.

**Action:** retain **Comrade Sanarthanam**.

This preserves political-movement address rather than flattening the relationship to generic “Mr.” or “friend.”

### 10. `குங்குமம்`

The wife's domestic bowl contains `மஞ்சள், குங்குமம்`.

**Action:** retain **turmeric and kumkum**.

**Kumkum** preserves the culturally specific substance without an unnecessary explanatory replacement.

### 11. Gold chain on the hand/wrist

The source repeatedly places the chain on `கை`, and P0/T2 established that English should not silently turn it into a necklace.

**Action:** retain **gold chain around my wrist** and **My dear boy's wrist is bare!**

The bodily placement is part of the mother's affection and the theft's material detail.

### 12. `சந்தனக் கிண்ணம்` — terminology correction

T1–T3 used **sandalwood bowl**, which can mean a bowl made from sandalwood.

The source sequence does not support that material reading. In the immediately following chapter, Kalaignar refers to the very same vessel as:

`அந்த வெள்ளிக் கிண்ணத்தையா உருக்கினார்கள்?`

— explicitly identifying it as a **silver bowl** that had been melted down.

Therefore `சந்தனக் கிண்ணம்` here denotes a bowl associated with sandal paste/use, not an object made of sandalwood.

**Action:** revise **sandalwood bowl** → **sandal-paste bowl** in both `v1-ch08` occurrences.

This is a source-supported terminology correction and is the audit's only chapter-text revision.

### 13. Student-movement and festival terminology

**inaugural celebration**, **annual festival**, **conference**, **movement**, **membership fees**, **travel money**, and the other organizational vocabulary remain ordinary English rather than over-formal institutional jargon.

**Action:** retain. The memoir's energy comes from concrete organizing activity, not bureaucratic nomenclature.

### 14. Cultural and domestic materiality

The chapter's **turmeric**, **kumkum**, gold chain, mother's box, pledge, interest, hotel meal, cinema and wife's bowl are not incidental decorations.

**Action:** retain them concretely. Do not generalize the two thefts into “selling family valuables” or “borrowing money.”
