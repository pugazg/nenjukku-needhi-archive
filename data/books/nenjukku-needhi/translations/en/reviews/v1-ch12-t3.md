2. **Advice to impatient writers** — `I sometimes speak even a little harshly like this` becomes **I sometimes speak this way—even a little harshly—...**
3. **1944 leaflet introduction** — the date sentence is tightened to **This Murasoli leaflet, printed and distributed on 5 October 1944, fiercely condemned it.**
4. **Historical-evidence exclamations** — `நான் கூறுவதல்ல! நாடறிந்த உண்மை! சரித்திரம் சாற்றும் சான்றுகளுடன்!` regains its oral force as **I am not the one saying this! It is a truth known to the country—with evidence proclaimed by history!**
5. **`நமக்கு வேலையில்லை` idiom** — `We have no work with savages` → **We have nothing to do with savages**.
6. **Varnashrama corpse sentence** — `dead corpse ... call its death a swoon` is made coherent as **lifeless corpse ... call it merely a faint**, preserving the source's corpse/fainting mockery.
7. **Friendship encouragement** — the sentence about close friends is made direct: they **stirred my heart and encouraged me to speak with feeling about friendship**.
8. **Political-awakening flag image** — `flag-hoisting for my present political awakening took place` becomes **the flag was hoisted for the political awakening I have today**, retaining `துவஜாரோகணம்`.
9. **1939 tense** — `I had been president` → **I was president**, preserving the authored 1939 date.
10. **Meeting-attendance cadence** — `people had to come` → **we needed people to come**, preserving the comic setup for chocolate distribution.
11. **Rival association / violence** — the Youth Association now **arose as a challenge to ours**, and the clashes **went so far that violence began**.
12. **Child-election English** — `aged eighteen and below` → **eighteen and under**; both sides **threw themselves into canvassing**, and the narrator **contested against his friend Thyagarasan**.
