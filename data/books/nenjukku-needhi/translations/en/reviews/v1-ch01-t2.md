## Review method

The complete Tamil chapter was compared against the fresh T1 by rhetorical unit, not by JSON extraction element. The review asked whether T1 retained what Kalaignar was doing — questioning, accumulating, comparing, joking, invoking, arguing and returning from public history to personal memory — as well as what he was saying.

## Findings and final T2 decisions

### 1. `பெரிய மனிதர்களுக்குத்தான் ... சின்னவர்களுக்குக் கிடையாதா?`

T1 used “great men” versus “ordinary people”. That conveyed the social meaning but weakened Kalaignar's deliberate `பெரிய` / `சின்ன` antithesis.

**Decision:** use “Does life — and history — belong only to the great? Have the small no share in them?” The slightly unusual “the small” is retained because the contrast itself carries Kalaignar's argument and is immediately intelligible from context.

### 2. Opening heart-dialogue

T1 over-explained `என் நெஞ்சே என்னைக் கேட்டுத்தான் முடித்தது` as though the heart kept questioning until it had finished.

**Decision:** tighten to the author's oral directness: it was not someone else asking; “my own heart asked me that question.” Preserve the following reversal that the heart also held the answer.

### 3. Life histories of ordinary people

The prisoners, elderly rickshaw pullers, sleepless mendicants and dying patient are not expendable illustrations. Together they establish Kalaignar's democratic theory of biography.

**Decision:** retain all examples and their escalation. Do not compress them during English polishing.

### 4. Storm and branch analogy

The analogy is the argument's hinge: an apparently small branch becomes part of history because it acts within a larger storm.

**Decision:** keep the question “the storm, or the branch?” and the conclusion that an event nevertheless enters the branch's history.

### 5. `என் நெஞ்சுக்கு நீதி`

The phrase is also the title-concept of the memoir.

**Decision:** retain “render justice to my heart” rather than replacing it with a smoother but less resonant paraphrase such as “justify writing my memoir”.

### 6. Rousseau quotation and response

T1 used “first sorrow” for `முதல் துன்பம்`.

**Decision:** use “first misfortune” as a closer rendering of Kalaignar's Tamil formulation. Do not replace the quotation with an external canonical English Rousseau wording.

### 7. Repetition around `நான் பிறந்த ஆண்டு இருக்கிறதே`

Kalaignar repeatedly turns back to “the year in which I was born” before expanding into history.

**Decision:** preserve the oral emphatic turn — “But the year in which I was born — that year...” — rather than smoothing every recurrence away.

### 8. 1924 accumulation

The repeated `காலம்` / `இந்த ஆண்டில்தான்` structure creates an oratorical historical roll-call.

**Decision:** retain repeated “It was a time when...” / “It was in that year...” structures. This is purposeful cadence, not verbosity to be edited out.

### 9. `உயிர் எமக்கு வெல்லமல்ல`

T1 flattened the concrete `வெல்லம்` image into “life was not so sweet”.

**Decision:** preserve the Tamil image directly: “Life is no jaggery to us!” The source-note sidecar explains the idiom; the literary text itself is not interrupted by an explanatory gloss.

### 10. `கால்கோள் விழா`

“Inaugural ceremony” weakened the foundation image.

**Decision:** use “as though the foundation stone were being laid for social justice and social revolution”.

### 11. `ஒரேயடியாகப் புரட்டிப் போட்ட`

T1 conveyed transformation but lost the force of doing it in one sweep.

**Decision:** restore “at a stroke” in the sentence about Arignar Anna overturning every sphere of Tamil life to meet the new age.

### 12. `தமிழ்த் தென்றல்` → `சண்டமாருதம்`

The Tamil moves from Kalyanasundara Mudaliar's epithet “Tamil Breeze” to a storm image against imperialism.

**Decision:** retain the epithet `Thamizh Thendral` and the answering “tempest” rather than reducing both to a neutral biographical description.

### 13. Question-mark self-irony

Kalaignar turns the historical uncertainty of 1924 into a joke about himself remaining a question mark to others.

**Decision:** retain the exclamation and self-mocking turn without explanation.

### 14. Gandhi prison hearsay markers

The source repeatedly uses `-ஆராம்` / reported-speech force for what Gandhi is said to have done in prison.

**Decision:** retain an “it is said” frame covering the sequence rather than converting reported material into unqualified narrator assertion.

### 15. `வெறித்தனமான பக்தி`

T1's “passionate devotion” softened Kalaignar's deliberately extreme self-description.

**Decision:** use “the almost-mad devotion I bear my leader Anna” in the comparison with Stalin's devotion to Lenin. The wording remains Kalaignar's self-characterisation, not an external judgement.

### 16. Gandhi and mass affection

T1 introduced “another power”, which was a summarising abstraction not required by the Tamil.

**Decision:** return to the concrete social point: in Gandhiji's case the masses displayed the special respect and immense affection society can show an individual out of love.

### 17. `நம்முடைய மாநிலம்`

T1 silently historicalised this as “our Presidency”.

**Decision:** translate what Kalaignar wrote: “our state”. Historical contextualisation, if ever needed, belongs in notes, not inside his sentence.

### 18. Cauvery/Hemavathi narrative time

T1 wrongly shifted the passage into retrospective past. The Tamil speaks from its own contemporary present: the agreement is to be reconsidered in 1974; Tamils are concerned; the Mysore Chief Minister has moved on Hemavathi; the Tamil Nadu Government has registered opposition.

**Decision:** restore the present/perfect tenses so the English preserves Kalaignar's historical vantage at the time of writing.

### 19. `நாள் பிறந்த 1924`

The extracted source has an apparent OCR/extraction form `நாள் பிறந்த` where the immediate context strongly indicates `நான் பிறந்த`.

**Decision:** translate the contextual reading “the 1924 in which I was born” and disclose the intervention in `source-notes/v1-ch01.json`; do not alter the read-only source.

### 20. `ராஜா` / `மந்திரி`

The line works through the domestic name “Raja” and the political title “Manthiri”.

**Decision:** retain both Tamil-derived words with unobtrusive English apposition — “Raja — king” / “Manthiri — minister” — so the joke survives.

### 21. Closing Thirukkuvalai image sequence

The Tamil deliberately changes into short visual bursts: tank; grove; temple; boundary shrines.

**Decision:** retain the fragments and exclamatory pulse instead of combining them into conventional English descriptive prose.
