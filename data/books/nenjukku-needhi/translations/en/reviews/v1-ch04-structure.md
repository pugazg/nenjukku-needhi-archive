## Governing question

## Structural map

### 1. A twelve-year-old breaches school authority

Kalaignar begins not with political theory but with a scene: the headmaster at work, a twelve-year-old rushing past the peon, and an abrupt demand for admission.

Function: establish urgency and the boy's refusal to accept institutional refusal.

English: **PRESERVED**, including the dramatic present.

### 2. The descending examinations explain the predicament

The headmaster asks why the boy has successively attempted Second Form, First Form and finally Fifth Standard. The boy blames the mismatch between his village teacher's promise and the preparation he actually received.

Function: turn what first looks like impudence into a concrete educational crisis involving family expectation and village honour.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 3. Refusal escalates into a suicide threat

The headmaster says there is no place. The boy answers that he will jump into the tank opposite and die before the headmaster's eyes.

Function: the child's determination moves from pleading to shocking ultimatum.

English: **PRESERVED WITHOUT SOFTENING**.

### 4. Kamalalayam gives the threat physical weight

The narration pauses over the famous tank: its size, moss-covered steps, accidental drownings and deliberate suicides. The headmaster is shaken because the threat is attached to a real and dangerous place.

Function: suspense comes from local physical memory, not from abstract melodrama.

English: **PRESERVED**. T3 naturalises `சுழன்றது` as the tank `swirls through his mind` without removing the image.

### 5. Hesitation turns into victory

The headmaster thinks, encourages the boy and gives him a place. The boy runs out shouting `Victory!`, embraces his waiting father and later passes with top marks.

Function: close the school-admission mini-drama on the child's will and vindication.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 6. Kalaignar delays the autobiographical reveal

Only after the incident is complete does the narrator ask whether the reader has understood who the boy was: `It was I myself!`

Function: the third-person anecdote becomes self-history through a delayed reveal.

English: **PRESERVED**. The translation does not identify Kalaignar early.

### 7. The Fifth Standard book becomes the political hinge

A roughly fifty-page supplementary book, `The Raja of Panagal`, is memorised by the young Kalaignar and becomes his `political primer`.

Function: the chapter's title metaphor `அரிச்சுவடி` converts school memory into the origin point of political education.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 8. Personal memory widens into Justice Party origins

Kalaignar moves directly from the primer to the leaders who came together in 1916, their aim of freeing the great Dravidian community from fetters, and the formation of the organisation rendered by its established English name, South Indian Liberal Federation.

Function: the boy's reading opens into the movement history that formed his political alphabet.

English: **PRESERVED WITHOUT ENCYCLOPEDIC REORDERING**.

### 9. Newspapers explain how the Justice name takes hold

The English `Justice`, Tamil `Dravidan` and Telugu `Andhra Prakasini` are introduced as organs for propagating party policy; the party gradually comes to be known by the English paper's name.

Function: explain the public identity `Justice Party` through movement communication.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 10. Representation becomes the first major political claim

The 1917 statement placed before Chelmsford and Montagu is contrasted with Home Rule plans and presented as revealing the real condition of Dravidian society, especially backward and depressed communities. Leaders from depressed communities then join and work through the party.

Function: move from organisation-building to the chapter's social-representation argument.

English: **PRESERVED**. T3 removes the translator-added hedge `in their view`, restoring Kalaignar's direct assertion.

### 11. Loss of Nair leads into electoral politics

The emergence of Sivaraj, M. C. Rajah and Rettamalai Srinivasan is followed by Dr Nair's death. The narrative then moves to the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms, the 1920 election, Home Rule opposition and Congress non-participation.

Function: leadership loss does not interrupt the movement story; it carries the narrative into constitutional politics.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 12. Electoral victory becomes ministry formation

The Justice Party's large victory leads to Thiagarayar being invited to form the ministry, his refusal of office for himself, the appointment of Subbarayalu Reddiar, Panagal Raja and K. V. Reddiar, and the Legislative Council presidency.

Function: show political representation becoming governmental power.

English: **PRESERVED**, with the period titles `First Minister` and `President of the Legislative Council` retained.

### 13. Panagal Raja's administration loops back to the schoolbook

After Subbarayalu's death, Panagal Raja becomes First Minister. Kalaignar then explicitly returns to the Fifth Standard book and says it was about the achievements secured for the Dravidians during Panagal Raja's tenure.

Function: prevent the historical section from floating free of autobiography; the political chronology is still the content of the child's `primer`.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 14. Labour repression reveals Justice Party confrontation with the Governor

The mill-workers' strike, six deaths and deportation order against five trade-union leaders lead to Thiagarayar's fury, the ministers' resignation letters and the Governor's withdrawal of the order.

Function: portray the party not only as office-holding but as willing to risk office in a confrontation over labour leaders.

English: **PRESERVED**, including the escalation from order to threatened resignation to withdrawal.

### 15. Successive elections and leadership deaths create instability

The second election and Panagal ministry are followed by Thiagarayar's death; the third election brings Swaraj Party victory, Congress refusal to form a ministry, Panagal Raja's refusal to govern without a majority, and Subbarayan's ministry. Panagal Raja then presses for provincial self-government and dies in 1928.

Function: political chronology becomes a narrative of repeated institutional contest and loss.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 16. Three deaths leave the Justice Party shaken

Nair, Thiagarayar and Panagal Raja have now disappeared one after another; the party `stood shaken`.

Function: compress cumulative leadership loss into a short physical image before the chapter pivots to Periyar.

English: **PRESERVED**. T3 removes only the awkward English `left standing shaken`, not the image.

### 17. Periyar introduces the Self-Respect / rationalist current

Kalaignar notes the Religious Endowments Protection Act, then moves to Periyar's departure from his role as a major Congress pillar, the founding of the Self-Respect Movement and the rationalist articles in `Kudi Arasu`.

Function: widen the political primer beyond Justice Party electoral history into the ideological stream that will reshape Dravidian politics.

English: **PRESERVED**.

### 18. A network spreads Self-Respect ideas

Jeevanandham, Alagirisami, Soundarapandian, Kuthoosi Gurusami, Ponnambalanar, Singaravelar, Kaivalya Swami, Ramanathan and others spread Self-Respect ideas at whirlwind speed.

Function: present the movement as a collective field of organisers rather than a single-leader history.

English: **PRESERVED WITHOUT NAME COMPRESSION**.

### 19. Anna enters as a student speaker

The 1930 Self-Respect Youth League becomes the point at which Anna, then a Pachaiyappa's College student, appears through weekly `சொற்போர்` and speeches.

Function: insert a later central figure into the political genealogy through youthful oratorical practice rather than retrospective grandeur.

English: **PRESERVED**, including the active `weekly verbal debates` register.

### 20. Justice Party and Periyar converge

After another Justice Party electoral victory and ministry, Periyar prepares a programme for Tamil political advancement, makes party acceptance a condition, the party accepts it, and he begins to work with the Justice Party.

Function: close the chapter on convergence between the institutional Justice Party stream and Periyar's political programme, completing the primer's historical arc.

English: **PRESERVED**.
