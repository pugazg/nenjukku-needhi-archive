## Source / lexical findings

### 1. `அரிநமோத்து சிந்தம்`

T1 correctly refused to invent a literal meaning, but left the phrase completely opaque.

Independent Tamil evidence establishes its **function** sufficiently for translation:

- another Tamil memoir describes `அரிநமோத்து சிந்தம்` being recited at a child's formal beginning of education immediately before the first letter is written in sand;
- the Tamil Digital Library text `தமிழலங்காரம்` preserves the same/near-identical traditional formula and connects it with an old invocation at the beginning of learning;
- other Tamil descriptions of older schooling likewise place the formula at the ceremonial commencement of writing.

The available evidence does not require us to silently Sanskritise Kalaignar's Tamil form or impose a speculative word-for-word doctrinal translation.

**T2 action:** retain `Arinamothu Sindham`, but identify it minimally in the sentence as an **old initiation formula**. Remove the approval-blocking lexical review item.

### 2. Postmaster Iyer's `நோக்கு`

T1 translated `தபால் வந்திருக்கு நோக்கு!` as `The mail's come, look!` by taking `நோக்கு` as the ordinary verb `look`.

This is incorrect in this speaker context. Tamil Lexicon evidence records `நோக்கு` as a dated Brahmin-Tamil form of `உனக்கு` (`for/to you`). The source explicitly identifies the speaker as `போஸ்ட் மாஸ்டர் அய்யர்`, making that dialectal reading contextually exact.

**T2 action:** render the call as `Mail has come for you! Send the boy over! I'll send it!` Do not caricature the dialect with an invented English regional accent.

### 3. `மூளியாக` in the Muneeswaran description

T1's `like a broken hulk` conveyed damage but added an image not present in the Tamil. Tamil Lexicon senses of `மூளி` include deformed, defective, or a thing with a part missing/broken.

**T2 action:** use the more source-near `ruined, faded, mutilated figure` and keep the following comic personification direct: `When his own lot is hard enough, how can he come to help others?`

### 4. `மாம்பழக் கதுப்பு`

Tamil Lexicon records `கதுப்பு` both as cheek and as the fleshy part of a fruit beside the seed, which is precisely what makes Kalaignar's comparison work.

T1's `fleshy curve of a mango` is understandable but imprecise.

**T2 action:** render `her cheeks like the fleshy sides of a mango`, preserving the concrete comparison rather than replacing it with generic `plump cheeks`.

## Kalaignar-voice findings

### 5. Opening ritual register — `வைதிகப் பெற்றோர்கள்`

`traditionally orthodox parents` is stiff and redundant.

**T2 action:** use `orthodox parents` while retaining the sequence of rites and the lightly amused retrospective tone.

### 6. Hair-pot punchline

T1's `Only after he opened it would he have discovered the news!` is too literal to English `செய்தி` and weakens the joke.

**T2 action:** `Only after he opened it would he have learned the truth!` This preserves the delayed comic revelation without explaining it.

### 7. `நாலு பெரிய மனிதர்கள்`

In colloquial Tamil `நாலு` here does not need to be read as an exact numerical count. More importantly, `பெரிய மனிதர்கள்` is the social-status phrase that matters.

T1's `If four 'important men' were present` sounds accidentally numerical and smooths the pointed `big men` language.

**T2 action:** `Where the 'big men' were gathered, I could not go before them wearing a shirt.` Preserve the quotes/irony and the social hierarchy.

### 8. `கடுமையான அடிமைத்தனம்`

T1's `harsh servitude` is somewhat softer than Kalaignar's political language.

**T2 action:** use `harsh bondage` and restore `கொடுமை` as `cruelty`, not merely `humiliation`.

### 9. Stones and thorns — `அதன் சுகத்தை`

T1 says one who walked over stones and thorns can describe `what that experience is like`, losing Kalaignar's sarcastic use of `சுகம்` (`pleasure`).

**T2 action:** restore the irony: `Only one who has walked over stones and thorns can describe the pleasure of it, can he not?`

### 10. Reform / revolutionary contrast

The concrete sequence is strong in T1, but `backward masses` can read as the translator's insult rather than the historical/social category.

**T2 action:** use `backward communities` in this passage. Preserve the unhealed wound + `punugu` perfume, surgical cure, kicked ball, and the repeated departure/rising of the oppressed person.

The final two Tamil clauses repeat `புறப்படு` and are more staccato than T1.

**T2 action:** `A man who comes from an oppressed people sets out to wipe away degradation. He sets out in fury.`

### 11. Tutor sentence

`A separate tutor too was arranged for me` is mechanical English.

**T2 action:** `A private tutor was arranged for me too.` Keep the period detail of payment in a seer of fresh milk and vegetables.

### 12. Nostalgia analogy

The long aged-couple analogy is intentionally expansive and should not be condensed. T1 preserves its function.

**Action:** retain the full analogy, with only the `கதுப்பு` lexical refinement noted above.

### 13. Artistic inexpressibility

T1 adds `greatest` in `Even the greatest master of art`, where Tamil simply says no `கலைவல்லார்` can express the feeling.

**T2 action:** remove the added superlative: `Even a master artist could not fully express such a feeling.`

### 14. `ஈயச் செம்பு`

T1 generalised this to `metal vessel`, losing a concrete village object. `ஈயம்` is lead.

**T2 action:** restore `a lead vessel`.

### 15. Mantra / belief / rationalist turn

The invocation remains transliterated; do not replace Kalaignar's remembered form with a standardised Sanskrit mantra. The later line about the belief's root being plucked out must remain as his retrospective ideological turn, not as translator commentary.

**Action:** T1 treatment largely passes.

### 16. Angalamman and curse-soil humour

The chicken-curry puja and soil blowing back into devotees' own eyes both pass. Do not add explanation after either joke.

### 17. `அரிநமோத்து சிந்தம்` school-memory line

T1's `until my fingers wore sore` is awkward and slightly weakens `தேயத் தேய`.

**T2 action:** `until my fingertips wore down`, while adding only the minimal functional gloss `old initiation formula`.

### 18. Village dead / oral catalogue

The repeated `இதோ` / `அதோ`, Vaidyalinga Mama's Garuda omen, `he himself has flown away`, Vaithi Thatha, Appa fishing, Kuppusami Thatha's keys, the two Avvaiyars, and the post office must remain a spoken catalogue rather than polished descriptive essay.

**Action:** preserve the repetition and fragments. Correct only the `நோக்கு` dialect error.

### 19. `உச்சிமோந்து`

T1's `smelled the crown of my head in affection` is unusual English but accurately preserves a specific Tamil gesture of affection. Tamil Lexicon explicitly defines the expression as smelling the crown of a child's head out of affection.

**Action:** retain rather than replacing it with the culturally different generic `kissed me`.

### 20. Final transition to Tiruvarur

The chapter's last turn—from villagers blessing the `son of Muthuvel` to the twelve-year-old leaving for school and then being refused even Fifth Standard admission—is the bridge into `v1-ch04`.

**Action:** preserve the abrupt closing setback; do not editorially foreshadow or explain the next chapter.
